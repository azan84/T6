# gosling-2020-sidebranch-ffr

- Authors: Rebecca C. Gosling, Jacob Sturdy (joint first authors, contributed equally), Paul D. Morris, Fredrik Eikeland Fossan, Leif Rune Hellevik, Patricia Lawford, D. Rodney Hose, Julian Gunn
- Year: 2020 (Accepted 18 February 2020)

- DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2020.109698
- Code URL: NOT REPORTED
- Data URL: NOT REPORTED — "Supplementary data to this article can be found online at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2020.109698" (Appendix A) is referenced (supplemental Table 1 on sensitivity to exponent c; supplemental Fig. 1 on vFFR error vs. CMVR deviation from Rmodel) but was not present in the extracted PDF text and is not a data repository.

## Problem & claim
- Imaging modality and domain: Invasive coronary angiography (two 2D angiographic acquisitions used to reconstruct 3D single-lumen vessel anatomy) plus invasively measured proximal/distal pressures (FFR); coronary artery disease hemodynamics.
- Task (segmentation / registration / detection / classification / generation / other): Other — reduced-order (1D) computational hemodynamics for virtual FFR (vFFR) prediction, structured as a controlled ablation: the same base 1D model with vs. without an added distributed "leakage" term representing unmodeled side-branch flow.
- Stated gap the paper attacks: "When constructing a geometric model of the coronary arteries, assumptions must be made about which branches and outlets should be represented explicitly. Prior work often assumes the flow to side branches is non-influential and models the artery of interest as a single lumen (Morris et al., 2013)... Tu et al. (2016) implicitly accounted for flow to side branches by assuming constant flow velocity; however, no comparison was made to evaluate the impact of this assumption. This study seeks to quantify the effect of neglecting side branch flow when predicting FFR and other indices of coronary physiology."
- Central claim in one sentence: "The results show that the leakage function did not significantly change the vFFR but did significantly impact the estimated volumetric flow rate and predicted coronary flow reserve. As both procedures achieved similar predictive accuracy of vFFR despite large differences in coronary blood flow, these results suggest careful consideration of the application of this index for quantitatively assessing flow." (Abstract)

## Method
- Architecture / algorithm, in enough detail to reimplement at a block level: Base model (1D): centreline radius sampled at 0.1 mm spacing from a reconstructed single-lumen 3D vessel; axisymmetric 1D reduced Navier–Stokes model (Fossan et al., 2018) under steady, laminar flow assumptions (Eq. 1.1, velocity-profile parameter f = 4.31, μ = 3.5e-3 Pa·s, ρ = 1050 kg/m³); an empirical stenosis pressure-loss term (Eq. 1.3, Liang et al. 2011 coefficients) applied where a stenosis-detection filter flags narrowing relative to an estimated "healthy" radius. Leakage extension (1Dleaky): Qin in Eq. 1.1 is varied along the centreline (Eq. 1.7) using a Murray's-law-derived flow-taper relationship (Eq. 1.4–1.6, exponent c = 2.4, sensitivity checked in supplemental Table 1) that infers the fraction of flow lost to unrepresented side branches from local vessel taper relative to an estimated healthy radius; if outlet radius exceeds inlet radius (taper inverted), no leakage is applied and 1Dleaky reduces to 1D ("if r(L) > r(0) the 1Dleaky model is identical to the 1D model"). Two boundary-condition protocols are run per model: (i) "Pressure matching computation of volumetric flow" — measured proximal (Pa) and distal (Pd) pressures imposed at inlet/outlet, flow (Q) solved for, then CMVR = Pd/Qout (Eq. 1.8) and stenotic segment resistance SR (Eq. 1.9, using mean of Qin/Qout) computed per case; (ii) "Minimally invasive prediction of vFFR" — measured Pa imposed at inlet, a fixed generic outlet resistance Rmodel applied (Pout = Rmodel·Qout), vFFR = Pout/Pa. An analogous baseline/hyperaemic pair of computations yields CFR and a minimally-invasive vCFR using generic baseline/hyperaemic CMVR values as boundary conditions.
- Key technical novelty (what specifically is new vs. the closest prior work): Adding a Murray's-law-based distributed leakage function to the previously published 1D model of Fossan et al. (2018), applied on the *same* reconstructed single-lumen geometry, and directly comparing it against the un-augmented model on identical cases — contrasted with Tu et al. (2016), which "implicitly accounted for flow to side branches by assuming constant flow velocity; however, no comparison was made to evaluate the impact of this assumption."
- Loss functions, training regime, augmentation, pre-training: NOT REPORTED / not applicable — deterministic reduced-order CFD model, no learned/trained component.
- Inputs/outputs, dimensionality, resolution: Input — centreline radius profile sampled at 0.1 mm spacing from a single-lumen 3D reconstruction derived from two 2D invasive angiographic projections, plus invasively measured proximal (aortic) and distal pressures. Output — per-artery vFFR (continuous 0–1, threshold ≤0.80), volumetric inflow/outflow (mL/s), CMVR, SR, CFR, vCFR, for 146 arteries in 80 patients.

## Data

Exclusions: "presented acutely within the previous 60 days, had previous coronary artery bypass graft surgery or chronic total occlusions, or were unable to provide consent." Private clinical cohort (UK, funded by BHF/NIHR/Wellcome Trust grants per Funding section); no licence stated; no public release.
- Annotation protocol, number of annotators, inter-rater agreement: "3D vessel anatomy was reconstructed with outlet corresponding to the location where distal pressure was measured from two 2D angiographic acquisitions and the segments of interest were extracted as a single lumen model via purpose built software." No annotator count, no inter-rater/inter-observer agreement metric for the reconstruction is reported anywhere in the text.
- Train/val/test split strategy; external validation present? (yes/no + which cohort): Not an ML train/val/test study. No held-out split — the same 146-artery/80-patient cohort is used both to calibrate the generic Rmodel constants (by averaging per-case CMVR from protocol (i)) and to evaluate vFFR diagnostic accuracy under protocol (ii). The authors flag this explicitly as a limitation (see Limitations). No external validation cohort; external validation = NOT REPORTED / no.

## Evaluation
- Metrics used, with exact reported values for the headline result:
  - vFFR vs FFR agreement: median vFFR 1D = 0.91 (0.82–0.95), 1Dleaky = 0.92 (0.81–0.96); "Including leakage increased vFFR by an average of 0.005 (95%CI 0.0, 0.01; p = 0.08)" — not significant. Correlation 1D vFFR–FFR = 0.65, 1Dleaky vFFR–FFR = 0.65, 1D–1Dleaky vFFR = 0.93.
  - Diagnostic accuracy (Table 2), FFR ≤ or > 0.80: 1D — Sensitivity 75%, Specificity 46%, PPV 95%, NPV 88%, AUC 0.84, Average error |FFR-vFFR| 0.10(0.09). 1Dleaky — Sensitivity 72%, Specificity 46%, PPV 91%, NPV 78%, AUC 0.82, Average error 0.11(0.10). Overall accuracy stated in text: "Overall diagnostic accuracy (ability to predict FFR ≤ or > 0.80) was similar for both models (1D 75%; 1Dleaky 72%)."
  - Bias/error: "Mean bias of vFFR was similar between 1D and 1Dleaky models (0.06(0.00–0.10) vs 0.07(0.00–0.11), p = 0.08). The 1Dleaky model had greater absolute error than the 1D model (0.08(0.04–0.13) vs 0.08(0.04–0.14), p = 0.02)." Bland-Altman: "Limits of agreements are −0.22–0.31, −0.20–0.29 and −0.12–0.12, respectively with mean biases of 0.046, 0.047, and −0.001 respectively" (1D vs FFR, 1Dleaky vs FFR, 1D vs 1Dleaky).
  - Flow: "The 1Dleaky model inflow was significantly higher (1.97(1.13–3.45) mL/s or 148%(125–190%) of 1D flow, p ≤ 0.001) and outflow was significantly lower (0.75(0.36–1.35) mL/s or 60%(47–77%) of 1D flow, p < 0.001)." Also stated: "The leakage function increased average inlet hyperaemic flow from 1.53 mL/s to 2.52 mL/s, (65% increase) and decreased outlet flow from 1.53 mL/s to 1.00 mL/s (35% decrease). On average, 56% of flow leaked from inlet to outlet." (Highest in LAD, 60%; lowest in RCA, 44%.)
  - CFR/vCFR: measured-pressure CFR not significantly different (1Dleaky 1.92 vs 1D 1.92, p = 0.236); vCFR (generic-CMVR-based) differed significantly (1Dleaky 2.91 vs 1D 2.74, p < 0.001).
  - Resistances: hyperaemic CMVR 1Dleaky significantly higher than 1D (ratio 1.86, p ≤ 0.001); SR ratio 0.91 (p < 0.001).
- Baselines compared against: The 1D model (Fossan et al., 2018) without leakage is the baseline; 1Dleaky is the intervention/ablation. External comparator for flow magnitude: thermodilution-measured flows from Xaplanteris et al. (2018) (Table 5).
- Statistical testing / confidence intervals / ablations present? Shapiro-Wilk (normality), robust linear regression with two-sided tests for non-zero differences (statsmodels), Bland-Altman analyses, sensitivity check on Murray's-law exponent c (supplemental Table 1), sensitivity of vFFR error to |CMVR − Rmodel| (supplemental Fig. 1). No formal sample-size/power calculation reported.
- Compute reported (GPU type, hours, params, inference time): NOT REPORTED.

## Limitations
- Limitations the authors admit (Section 4.4, verbatim): "The accuracy of computed FFR results are affected by the accuracy of the reconstruction and assumptions about the boundary conditions. The generic resistance applied at the distal boundary when computing vFFR limits case-specific accuracy. Further, since this resistance was calibrated using all cases, the predictive accuracy may be worse if this value is applied to future cases. However, the same assumptions were made in each model, allowing accurate between-model comparison; the primary aim of this study. The clinical data were obtained under ideal circumstances in elective cases; however, no invasive flow measurements were acquired for comparison of computed flow values. Without flow measurements, comparison with previous studies is complicated and individual accuracy is unknown. A lack of information about larger side branches prohibits direct comparison of the leakage model with models that explicitly include side branches. The assumption that vessel taper corresponds to flow to side branches neglects the possibility that taper may reflect the presence of concentric atheroma, which may invalidate Murray's law. Some 1Dleaky inflows are unphysiologically large (e.g. 17.5 mL/s), which likely occurs when taper does not correspond to Murray's law. Stenoses at the inlet of the artery and tapering due to disease may both cause the tapering ratio to misrepresent the flow to side branches, an appropriate methodology to identify abnormal taper from healthy taper would be valuable in further investigations. Further, flow to side branches is likely distributed non-uniformly and a leakage model accounting for variations could be explored. Side branch flow likely affects diffuse lesions differently than focal lesions; however, as the data are not annotated to differentiate these cases, this hypothesis was not evaluated. Further, the heuristic stenosis detection algorithm purports to identify regions where the 1D assumptions are violated and thus may not necessary mark all diseased regions or may break the diseased region into multiple regions. Comparison of stenotic flow between models would require an appropriate method to define which flow should be compared between the models. The segment resistance, defined based on average flow, provides one point of comparison."
- Limitations I can see that they do NOT admit: (1) Rmodel is calibrated on the same 146-artery cohort used to report diagnostic accuracy — a form of in-sample circularity beyond what the authors' own admission ("predictive accuracy may be worse if this value is applied to future cases") frames as a generalization concern rather than a circularity concern for the reported accuracy numbers themselves. (2) No case-level reclassification analysis is performed or reported despite sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV all shifting numerically between models (e.g., NPV 88%→78%), which could in principle reflect individual arteries crossing the 0.80 threshold — this is never quantified. (3) The paper does not investigate geometric/topological segmentation error (missed bifurcations, spurious branches, wrong connectivity) at all — only a physiological under-modeling assumption on an otherwise fixed, correct single-lumen anatomy — so the generalizability of "diagnostic accuracy is insensitive to this compensating mechanism" to genuine anatomical/topological error is untested and unaddressed even implicitly.

## Openings
- Explicit "future work" statements, quoted: "an appropriate methodology to identify abnormal taper from healthy taper would be valuable in further investigations." "flow to side branches is likely distributed non-uniformly and a leakage model accounting for variations could be explored." "Side branch flow likely affects diffuse lesions differently than focal lesions; however, as the data are not annotated to differentiate these cases, this hypothesis was not evaluated." "Comparison of stenotic flow between models would require an appropriate method to define which flow should be compared between the models. The segment resistance, defined based on average flow, provides one point of comparison." "The increasing adoption of computed physiological indices may require better understanding of the significance of both relative and absolute measures of coronary artery function."
- 3–6 unresolved questions this paper leaves open, each one sentence:
  1. Whether the same insensitivity of vFFR diagnostic accuracy holds when side branches are represented by genuine anatomical/topological errors (missed bifurcations, spurious branches, wrong connectivity) rather than by a physiological leakage term on an otherwise correct single-lumen geometry — never tested here.
  2. Whether individual arteries reclassify across the FFR ≤0.80 threshold between the 1D and 1Dleaky models, given that sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV all shift numerically but no case-level crossing count is reported.
  3. Whether a per-case (rather than cohort-averaged) distal resistance would change the conclusion that diagnostic accuracy is preserved despite large flow differences.
  4. Whether the taper-based leakage estimate remains valid in diffusely diseased vessels, since the data were not annotated to separate diffuse from focal lesions.
  5. Whether the generic Rmodel, calibrated on the same 146-artery cohort used for accuracy reporting, would still yield similar accuracy on an independent, unseen cohort.
  6. Whether accounting for larger, explicitly-modeled side branches (rather than a distributed leakage proxy) would alter the finding, given the authors' own statement that "a lack of information about larger side branches prohibits direct comparison of the leakage model with models that explicitly include side branches."

## T6 decisive questions

**1. Geometric change 1D vs. 1Dleaky — physical branch added/removed, or distributed leakage on an unchanged geometry?**
Decisively: the geometry is unchanged. Both models use the identical single-lumen reconstruction. Methods, §2.3: "3D vessel anatomy was reconstructed with outlet corresponding to the location where distal pressure was measured from two 2D angiographic acquisitions and the segments of interest were extracted as a single lumen model via purpose built software." §2.5 (Leakage model) states the motivation explicitly: "Because the reconstructed anatomy neglects side branches, the assumption that inflow equals outflow (Qin = Qout) in the main vessel is invalid. To extend the 1D model, the flow Qin (1.1) is varied along the centreline to account for flow to branches which are not explicitly represented." No side branch is added to or removed from the geometric model at any point — the leakage term is a mathematical flow-redistribution function (Murray's-law taper-based, Eq. 1.4–1.7) applied along the centreline of the *same* single-lumen vessel used by the 1D model. This is categorically different from adding/removing an anatomical branch or changing topology/connectivity.

**2. How Rmodel/CMVR is determined — quote, values, per-case or averaged?**
Per-case CMVR (setting i, pressure-matching): "CMVR = Pd/Qout" (Eq. 1.8), computed for each case from invasively measured Pd and the model-computed Qout. But the boundary condition actually used for vFFR prediction (setting ii) is a single, cohort-averaged constant, not case-specific: "In this study a model-specific resistance (Rmodel) is applied as the distal boundary condition such that Pout = RmodelQout (see Fig. 1). The value of Rmodel is the average of all CMVR values calculated from the volumetric flow in setting (i) for the given model (1D = 1.23e+10 Pa/m3 s−1 and 1Dleaky = 2.42e+10 Pa/m3 s−1)." An analogous pair of generic constants is used for baseline vCFR: "(1D = 2.83e+10 Pa/m3 s−1 and 1Dleaky = 5.95e+10 Pa/m3 s−1)." It is explicitly a single averaged constant per model variant, applied identically to every patient — confirmed in Discussion 4.1: "the outlet by a generic, model-specific resistance value representing the CMVR and tuned to the model, but not to the individual patient," and again in Limitations 4.4: "since this resistance was calibrated using all cases, the predictive accuracy may be worse if this value is applied to future cases."

**3. The "obviating differences in predicted FFR" passage — full quote and mechanism.**
Full passage (Discussion §4.1): "In our model, the inlet is defined as the proximal aortic pressure and the outlet by a generic, model-specific resistance value representing the CMVR and tuned to the model, but not to the individual patient. Determination of patient specific CMVR requires a pressure wire in the distal position. Lower outflow in the 1Dleaky model compared with the 1D model results in a higher average CMVR, which effectively tunes the boundary condition to achieve similar predictive accuracy for FFR. Thus the tuning of boundary conditions may obviate differences in predicted FFR that are expected if models predict flows quite different from the unknown physical flow. Moreover, as FFR depends upon the relative pressures, as a proxy for relative flow, the value of predicted FFR can be identical for models with different volumetric flows." Mechanism claimed: because vFFR = Pout/Pa and Pout = Rmodel·Qout, a model variant that computes systematically lower Qout (1Dleaky's side-branch leakage removes flow from the main-vessel outlet) is compensated by re-averaging Rmodel upward (because Rmodel is derived from the same cohort's Pd/Qout), so the *product* Rmodel·Qout — and hence Pout/Pa — lands close to the value obtained by the un-leaked model with its own, lower, averaged Rmodel. It is a ratio/re-normalization argument at the level of a single fitted cohort-wide constant, not a per-patient re-tuning to hit an individual target.

**4. Per-case FFR reclassification across 0.80, or only aggregate metrics?**
Only aggregate metrics are reported. Table 2 gives Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV, NPV, AUC, correlation, and average error per model (1D: 75%/46%/95%/88%/0.84/0.65/0.10; 1Dleaky: 72%/46%/91%/78%/0.82/0.65/0.11), and the text states "Overall diagnostic accuracy... was similar for both models (1D 75%; 1Dleaky 72%)." No sentence, table, or figure anywhere in the extracted text reports a per-case/per-artery count of how many of the 146 arteries changed side of the FFR ≤0.80 threshold between the 1D and 1Dleaky models (Fig. 2A is described only as a scatter plot with a correlation coefficient and a dashed threshold line, with no crossing count given). Plainly: it is aggregate sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV/AUC/accuracy only — case-level reclassification counts are NOT REPORTED.

**5. Framing — masking as a PROBLEM (validation hazard) or a CONVENIENCE (justification for simpler models)?**
The paper does not frame the tuning-driven insensitivity as a validation hazard/warning that clinicians or modelers could be misled by masked error. Its framing is closer to a methodological caution specifically scoped to *flow-based* indices, while treating the FFR-insensitivity finding itself in a neutral-to-reassuring register for FFR purposes. Abstract: "As both procedures achieved similar predictive accuracy of vFFR despite large differences in coronary blood flow, these results suggest careful consideration of the application of this index for quantitatively assessing flow." Conclusion: "The addition of a leakage term had no significant effect on the predictive accuracy of vFFR despite significant differences in the estimated volumetric flow rate. The insensitivity of accuracy of predicted vFFR to differences in predicted flow may reflect the fact that FFR is a ratio whereas absolute measures of flow will be directly affected. The increasing adoption of computed physiological indices may require better understanding of the significance of both relative and absolute measures of coronary artery function." And explicitly reassuring toward the simpler (non-leaky) model for FFR purposes: "Thus, compensating for side-branch flow does not appear to improve the prediction of FFR but is an important consideration when estimating flow rate and associated indices such as predicted CFR." Net framing: convenience/reassurance for vFFR-only use of simpler single-lumen models, paired with a caution restricted to flow-quantification indices (CFR/vCFR) — not a general validation-hazard framing about whether matching outputs can hide wrong models.

**6. Does the paper address TOPOLOGICAL error (wrong connectivity, missed bifurcation, spurious branch)?**
No. The word "topolog-" does not appear anywhere in the extracted text, and no passage discusses connectivity errors, missed bifurcations, or spurious branches. The entire analysis concerns under-representation of side-branch *flow* on an anatomically fixed, correctly-connected single-lumen geometry — a physiological/hemodynamic modeling-assumption question, not a geometric/topological segmentation-error question. This is stated plainly by the Limitations section's own scoping: "A lack of information about larger side branches prohibits direct comparison of the leakage model with models that explicitly include side branches" — i.e., they acknowledge not modeling explicit branches at all, but never raise or test the possibility of an anatomically *wrong* (missegmented) tree.

**7. Stated limitations, verbatim.**
See "## Limitations" above (Section 4.4 quoted in full).

**8. DECISIVE JUDGEMENT — how much of T6's headline is already in print here?**
Gosling et al. (2020) already demonstrates, in print, the general *mechanism* T6 needs: that a fixed, cohort-averaged distal-resistance boundary condition (Rmodel/CMVR), because it is derived from the same model variant's own average flow, re-normalizes so that a model with substantially different absolute flow (here, ~65% higher inflow, ~35% lower outflow, ~56% of flow "leaked") produces statistically indistinguishable *aggregate* vFFR diagnostic accuracy (75% vs. 72%; AUC 0.84 vs. 0.82, not significantly different) — and the authors state this mechanism explicitly: "the tuning of boundary conditions may obviate differences in predicted FFR that are expected if models predict flows quite different from the unknown physical flow." This is a real, citable precedent for the *qualitative* claim that boundary-condition tuning can absorb a large model-input change without moving the aggregate diagnostic-accuracy needle.

However, on every dimension that makes T6's headline specifically about *topological segmentation error*, Gosling does **not** pre-empt it, and reviewers should not be able to sustain a "this is already shown" objection if T6 is precise about the distinction:
- **No geometry/topology changes at all.** Both models share one identical single-lumen reconstruction; the "leakage" is a distributed physiological flow-redistribution term (Murray's-law taper), not an added, removed, or mis-connected branch. T6's claim is about anatomy being *wrong* (missed bifurcation, spurious branch, wrong connectivity); Gosling's manipulation leaves the anatomy untouched and only changes a flow-accounting assumption layered on top of it. This is the single most important distinction and Gosling states its own boundary explicitly (never uses "topology," never discusses connectivity/segmentation error).
- **No per-case re-tuning.** Rmodel is one constant per model variant, averaged over the *entire* 146-artery cohort and applied identically to every patient ("tuned to the model, but not to the individual patient"). T6's claim, as framed, concerns case-specific retuning of outlet boundary conditions to hit an individual patient's outlet pressure/flow targets after a geometry change — a fundamentally different, per-case adaptive mechanism that Gosling neither implements nor discusses.
- **No case-level reclassification evidence.** Gosling reports only aggregate sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV/AUC; it never reports how many individual arteries flipped across the 0.80 threshold. T6's headline explicitly needs the individual-level flip-while-aggregate-looks-fine result, which is exactly the analysis Gosling omits (despite PPV/NPV shifting numerically, e.g., NPV 88%→78%, suggesting some cases likely did move — but this is never quantified).
- **Framing is not a validation-hazard framing.** Gosling frames its finding as a reassurance for FFR modeling (simpler single-lumen models are adequate for FFR, though not for flow/CFR quantification), not as a warning that outlet-matched validation can conceal a wrong model — the epistemological claim T6 needs to make novel.

**Bottom line:** Gosling 2020 is a genuine, close, and citable prior finding for the *general principle* that boundary-condition re-tuning can absorb a substantial model-input discrepancy while leaving aggregate FFR diagnostic accuracy statistically unchanged — T6 must cite and directly distinguish itself from this paper, and cannot claim that principle itself as novel. But T6's specific headline — topological (segmentation/connectivity) error, absorbed by *per-case* re-tuning to match outlet targets, revealed by *individual-case* threshold flips despite preserved aggregate accuracy, and framed as a *validation hazard* — is not shown here on any of those four counts. What would remain genuinely new for T6 is precisely: (a) using actual topological/segmentation errors rather than a physiological leakage term on unchanged anatomy, (b) case-specific rather than cohort-averaged boundary-condition retuning, (c) reporting per-case reclassification counts across the 0.80 threshold, and (d) framing the result as a threat to validation practice rather than a reassurance about model simplification.
