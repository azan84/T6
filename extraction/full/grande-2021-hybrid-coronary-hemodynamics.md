## Problem & claim
- Imaging modality and domain: Coronary CT angiography (CTA)-derived anatomy, used only to build ONE baseline coronary tree geometry ("the normal coronary case available in www.simvascular.com"); no patient cohort imaging used for the study's actual test cases.
- Task (segmentation / registration / detection / classification / generation / other): Other — computational method development: a coupled 1D (reduced-order) / 3D (full CFD) hybrid hemodynamic simulation framework, validated by internal comparison against a full-3D CFD reference (not against any measured/invasive data).
- Stated gap the paper attacks: "1D models do not provide a spatially resolved description of the local flow conditions, i.e., the mechanical and biochemical environment, in the vicinity of the stenotic lesion where the thrombus may eventually form... On the other hand, 3D submodels focused on the stenotic region alone do not incorporate time-dependent patient-specific hemodynamic information." Also: "3D flow simulations of the whole coronary tree involve a high computational cost. Therefore, they could become a bottleneck considering cases with a significantly wide range of scales or time-evolving problems, such as thrombosis."
- Central claim in one sentence: "We validated our 1D–3D model against full 3D coronary simulations in healthy and diseased conditions. Our results showed good agreement between the 3D and the 1D–3D models while reducing the computational cost by 40-fold compared to the 3D simulation."

## Method
- Architecture / algorithm, in enough detail to reimplement at a block level:
  1. **3D CFD reference model**: baseline healthy coronary tree built in SimVascular from the "normal coronary case" reference geometry on www.simvascular.com; a 90% area-reduction stenosis is then virtually introduced in the LAD, "positioned at 2 cm from the bifurcation, between two healthy segmentations of the native vessel set 1 cm apart," with radius interpolated between the prescribed 2D segmentations directly in SimVascular. Tetrahedral FE mesh via TetGen, 3-layer boundary layer (70% layer-decreasing ratio), local refinement "from the proximal section of the stenosis to a distal point at least ten diameters downstream" (citing Young, ref 44). Max edge size 0.06 cm (global), 0.025 cm (local refinement), from a mesh-independence analysis. Navier–Stokes (incompressible Newtonian, ρ=1060 kg/m³, μ=0.004 kg/m⁻¹s⁻¹), rigid wall, solved with SimVascular's 3D FE flow solver for fully resolved u(X,t), p(X,t).
  2. **1D model**: centerlines and luminal diameters extracted from the same 3D anatomical model; each coronary segment discretized into 1D FEs, element size 0.1 cm. 1D mass/momentum equations (Eqs. 4–5) integrated over the cross-section from 3D Navier–Stokes; constitutive law from Laplace's Law (Eq. 7) with E=1.15 MPa and an enforced rigidity multiplier Cr=1.64 (tuned via numerical experiments to make the 1D wall effectively rigid, matching the rigid-wall 3D reference). An algebraic trans-stenotic pressure-drop closure (Seeley & Young, Eq. 8–9) is added to the 1D momentum equation at the stenosis because "the 1D flow assumption results in an underestimation of the viscous and turbulent losses." Solved via FEM (Wan et al. formulation) with Trilinos sparse solvers, Δt=1e-3 s.
  3. **Hybrid 1D–3D coupling**: "A 1D model of the global coronary tree was obtained by extracting the vessel centerlines and a 3D model of the region of interest (stenosis) was obtained via direct image segmentation in SimVascular." 3D sub-model mesh built with the same parameters as the full-3D model (boundary layer + local stenosis refinement).
  4. **0D closed-loop boundary conditions**: an LPN representing heart + pulmonary circulation + coronary microcirculation + a 3-element Windkessel (RCR) for the systemic circulation, coupled numerically to whichever flow solver (1D, 3D, or hybrid) is in use; identical LPN used across all three model types.
- Key technical novelty (what specifically is new vs. the closest prior work): "Here, we present the first 1D–3D coupled model of the coronary circulation." Prior hybrid 1D–3D work (Urquiza et al., Blanco et al.) targeted cerebral aneurysms, abdominal/femoral aorta, and carotid arteries — this is the first application of that coupling strategy to coronary flow, combined with a closed-loop heart+coronary-microcirculation LPN so that flow conditions "can be updated as the geometry changes" dynamically (as opposed to "a simple prescribed inlet flow obtained from a one-time 3D simulation").
- Loss functions, training regime, augmentation, pre-training: NOT REPORTED — not a learned/trained model; this is a numerical PDE coupling scheme.
- Inputs/outputs, dimensionality, resolution: Input — one CTA-derived healthy reference coronary geometry (SimVascular sample case) plus virtually-introduced stenosis geometries. Outputs — pulsatile flow Q(x,t) and pressure p(x,t) fields (1D and 3D), FFR (scalar per case), TAWSS, TASR (time-averaged shear rate), TASRg (shear rate gradient), and maximum WSS, all reported "at the location of the stenosis," defined as "the section of the coronary artery with minimum diameter."

### Coupling scheme (verbatim) — Question 1
Section "Hybrid 1D–3D Model": "Our 1D fluid solver can be used either independently or coupled to a 3D solver. In this work, we interfaced our 1D solver with the SimVascular 3D finite element solver. A 1D model of the global coronary tree was obtained by extracting the vessel centerlines and a 3D model of the region of interest (stenosis) was obtained via direct image segmentation in SimVascular. The extent to of the local 3D model was defined from the proximal section of the stenosis to a distal point at least ten diameters downstream. The 1D and 3D models were coupled numerically (using semi-implicit coupling) by imposing continuity of mass and total stress at the coupling interface:

σ(u3D, p3D) · n = p1D · n on Γb   (Eq. 10)
∫_Γb u3D · n dΓ = q1D            (Eq. 11)

where u3D, p3D and u1D, p1D refer to the velocity and pressure in the 3D and 1D model respectively, σ(u3D,p3D) is the total stress tensor defined as σ(u3D,p3D) = −p3D I + τ, q1D is the flow in the 1D model, Γb denotes the coupling surface, and n is the coupling surface normal. The coupling is enforced by applying a Newmann [sic] type boundary condition on the 3D model boundaries, which is computed based on the 1D model solution. We use a similar approach to that described in Esmaily Moghadam et al. First the flow rate is passed from the 3D to the 1D model, then pressure is passed from the 1D to the 3D domain via Eq. (10). Pressure and flow rate are exchanged between the 3D and 1D domain at each non-linear iteration."

Region assignment: the **global coronary tree** (all branches, 9 outlets in the reference case: 6 left, 3 right) is solved as **1D**; only the **region of interest containing the stenosis** is solved as **3D**, extracted "via direct image segmentation in SimVascular." Placement of the 3D region is determined a priori by where the lesion/stenosis of interest is located, not by an automated or geometry-driven criterion: "The framework is flexible and allows any section of the coronary tree to be replaced with a 3D representation," and cases are shown "where the stenosis is isolated in a segment of the LAD, even including a bifurcation as the 3D region of interest." Extent within that region is fixed by a rule of thumb ("at least ten diameters downstream" of the stenosis, citing Young 1979) rather than derived per-case; a sensitivity analysis on this extent showed "including a longer portion of the 3D model of the region of interest (i.e. stenosis) did not change substantially the results of the simulation... no significant improvement or worsening of the WSS estimation as a result of modifying the extent of the 3D sub-model," and the extra compute cost of a longer 3D region was "negligible respect to the full 3D computation time" (Electronic Supplementary Material [ESM] Section 4, not in extracted text).

## Limitations
- Limitations the authors admit (verbatim):
  - "The 1D model assumes unidirectionality of the flow, however, this does not hold in the presence of a stenosis. The drastic diameter reduction could induce vortices and secondary flows downstream and therefore velocity components in other directions are no longer negligible. This typically results in an underestimation of the pressure drop through the stenosis."
  - "the estimation of trans-stenotic pressure drop, in this case, relies on a simplified geometric characterization of the focal lesion—length and minimum cross-sectional area—which may not fully represent the complexity of atherosclerotic lesions."
  - "these differences may result from the curvature of the coronary arteries and the branching, which are not considered in the 1D representation... Future studies should include the effect of the curvature of the vessel curvature, in particular, upstream to the 3D model."
  - "We observed differences in the velocity profile proximal to the lesion comparing the 3D and the 1D–3D models. We believe these discrepancies may be the source of the WSS and shear rate errors. Future work should consider a correction of the inlet velocity profile based on the vessel curvature or proximity to a bifurcation."
  - "Follow up studies including patient specific coronary anatomies will focus on demonstrating that this modeling approach is prepared to deal with the complexities that may arise in a patient-specific model where atherosclerotic arteries can present diffuse lesions and/or lesions near bifurcations, as long as image segmentation is possible." (i.e., the authors themselves flag that real patient-specific/diffuse-disease anatomy has not yet been tested.)
  - "However, 1D flow simulations do not provide local hemodynamic data to inform a thrombosis model."
- Limitations I can see that they do NOT admit:
  1. The entire validation uses a single reference anatomy (one healthy SimVascular sample geometry) with virtually inserted, idealized (symmetric/asymmetric, single-length-varied) stenoses — there is no real diseased patient geometry, no cohort, and no test of anatomical diversity (tortuosity, calcification, diffuse/multifocal disease, bifurcation lesions in real patients) despite the paper's own framing as "patient-specific."
  2. There is no comparison to any measured/invasive ground truth (no catheter-based FFR, no invasive pressure wire, no in-vivo or ex-vivo WSS measurement) anywhere in the paper — all "validation" is hybrid/1D vs. the authors' own full-3D CFD of the same synthetic geometry, so the reported errors bound only the 1D-reduction error, not real-world accuracy.
  3. The quantitative WSS-specific accuracy reporting is thin and largely qualitative ("good agreement," "qualitatively similar," both "over 100 Pa") — the only precise WSS/shear-rate numeric error figures given are for **peak shear rate** (9% and ~10%), not for TAWSS or maxWSS values themselves, and no spatial-map error metric (e.g., pointwise correlation, Dice-like overlap of elevated-WSS regions, RMSE of the WSS field) is reported for WSS the way RMSE/R are reported for flow and pressure waveforms in Table 2.
  4. Boundary-condition parameters (cardiac output, aortic pressure) were fixed at "healthy population average values," not derived from any patient-specific data in this study, despite the text noting parameters "can be estimated based on patient clinical data" — so the demonstrated pipeline has not itself been run with patient-specific BCs.
  5. The 25% resistance reduction for hyperemic conditions is stated as "expected for a healthy vascular bed" (citing one reference), not validated against a diseased/CAD population, yet is used unchanged in the diseased-stenosis case.

## Openings
- Explicit "future work" statements, quoted: "Future studies should include the effect of the curvature of the vessel curvature, in particular, upstream to the 3D model." "Future work should consider a correction of the inlet velocity profile based on the vessel curvature or proximity to a bifurcation." "This framework also offers the possibility to solve reaction-advection-diffusion equations simultaneously with the flow... Future work will be focused on this direction, including patient-specific information regarding blood biochemistry to analyze the effect of platelet-count and other blood patient-specific biochemistry in the formation of the clot." "Follow up studies including patient specific coronary anatomies will focus on demonstrating that this modeling approach is prepared to deal with the complexities that may arise in a patient-specific model where atherosclerotic arteries can present diffuse lesions and/or lesions near bifurcations, as long as image segmentation is possible."
- 3–6 unresolved questions this paper leaves open, each one sentence:
  1. Whether the hybrid model's accuracy holds on real, patient-specific diseased coronary anatomies (diffuse lesions, bifurcation lesions, tortuosity), since every case here uses one healthy reference geometry with virtually inserted idealized stenoses.
  2. Whether the "40-fold" and "good agreement" claims would survive comparison against actual invasive/measured hemodynamic or WSS data rather than only against the authors' own full-3D CFD of the same synthetic geometry.
  3. Whether the demonstrated pipeline can be driven by patient-specific (not population-average) boundary-condition parameters without degrading the reported accuracy, since this study never actually did so.
  4. Whether the noted proximal velocity-profile discrepancy (flagged by the authors as the likely source of WSS/shear-rate error) can be corrected, and how much residual WSS error would remain afterward.
  5. Whether the method scales to a multi-case cohort at all, given that runtime/mesh/BC setup here (SimVascular geometry construction, TetGen meshing, LPN parameterization) was demonstrated for one anatomy only.

## Triage
- verdict: FULL
- reason: T6 secondary-arm cross-fidelity method candidate (1D/0D vs. 3D WSS/OSI generalization test); direct relevance to cost-feasibility of a WSS/OSI arm.

## T6 feasibility questions

1. **Coupling scheme.** The global coronary tree (all outlets, e.g. 9 in the reference case) is solved as 1D; only a pre-identified region of interest containing the stenosis is solved as full 3D, extracted "via direct image segmentation in SimVascular," extending "from the proximal section of the stenosis to a distal point at least ten diameters downstream." The interface enforces continuity of mass and total stress: "σ(u3D,p3D)·n = p1D·n on Γb" and "∫_Γb u3D·n dΓ = q1D," via "semi-implicit coupling" with a Neumann-type BC on the 3D boundary computed from the 1D solution, flow rate passed 3D→1D and pressure passed 1D→3D "at each non-linear iteration," following Esmaily Moghadam et al. Placement of the 3D region is **not** determined by an automated/geometric criterion — it is chosen a priori to contain the lesion under study ("The framework is flexible and allows any section of the coronary tree to be replaced with a 3D representation"); using it for a new case requires first knowing/segmenting where the lesion is.

2. **Speedup.** "Reducing the computational cost by 40-fold compared to the 3D simulation," specifically for the **diseased (LAD 90% stenosis) case**: 4314.24 core-hours (full 3D) vs. 107.2 core-hours (hybrid 1D–3D) ≈ 40.2×. The healthy-case ratio is larger: 1516.67 vs. 33.2 core-hours ≈ 45.7×. The pure 1D model is far cheaper still (0.91–0.93 core-hours) but that is not the "40-fold" figure. Hardware (CPU model, cluster, node count) is **NOT REPORTED** anywhere in the text — only "Core-hours = total hours × number of cores" is defined.

3. **WSS/OSI outputs.** Yes, it computes WSS-family quantities — "time-averaged wall shear stress (TAWSS), time-averaged shear rate (TASR), shear rate gradient (TASRg), and maximum WSS at the location of the stenosis" — but **only over the 3D sub-domain** (stenosis region ± ≥10 diameters downstream), never over the 1D-only tree. **OSI is never computed or mentioned as an output anywhere in the paper**; the single occurrence of "oscillatory" is a background-motivation sentence in the Introduction, not a result. T6 would get WSS but would have to add OSI computation itself (straightforward from the same 3D velocity field if the necessary time-history data is retained, but not something this paper provides or validates).

4. **Hybrid-vs-3D WSS accuracy.** Precise numeric error is reported only for a single derived quantity, peak shear rate: "9% higher in the full 3D simulation" (baseline stenosis) and "~10%" for asymmetric/long-stenosis variants. TAWSS/maxWSS themselves are compared only qualitatively ("qualitatively similar," "good agreement," both "over 100 Pa" with no matched numeric values or % difference given). No spatial-map error metric (pointwise correlation, RMSE, or overlap of elevated-WSS regions) is reported for the WSS field, unlike the RMSE/R given for flow and pressure waveforms in Table 2. This is a materially thinner accuracy characterization than the paper's framing ("comparable to the 3D simulation") implies.

5. **Boundary conditions.** Closed-loop LPN (heart + pulmonary circulation + coronary microcirculation + 3-element Windkessel/RCR systemic model), identical across 1D/3D/hybrid. "LPN model parameters can be estimated based on patient clinical data, such as cardiac output or aortic pressure" — in principle re-derivable — but in this study they were fixed at "healthy population average values for cardiac output and aortic pressure," with only "the coronary artery outlet cross-sectional area" taken "from the baseline coronary tree" (i.e., geometry-derived and thus re-derivable after a geometry change). The hyperemic 25% microvascular-resistance reduction is a fixed literature-based assumption ("expected for a healthy vascular bed"), not patient- or geometry-derived, and was applied unchanged to the diseased case too.

6. **Cohort.** Effectively **one** anatomy: a single healthy reference CTA-derived coronary tree ("the normal coronary case available in www.simvascular.com") with virtually inserted idealized stenoses (baseline symmetric 90%, a longer symmetric 90%, an asymmetric 90%) plus a separate idealized carotid-artery check (ESM Section 3). **No real patient data, no cohort, and no invasive ground truth of any kind** (no catheter FFR, no pressure-wire measurement, no in-vivo WSS) is used anywhere.

7. **Meshing.** Requires a segmentable CTA-derived lumen (SimVascular pipeline), centerline + diameter extraction for the 1D tree, and a TetGen tetrahedral mesh for the 3D sub-domain (3-layer boundary layer, 70% decreasing ratio, local refinement to 0.025 cm at the stenosis vs. 0.06 cm elsewhere, from a mesh-independence study). The paper makes **no robustness claim** for poor/real-world geometry; it explicitly defers this to future work, conditioning it on segmentability: "as long as image segmentation is possible," for "patient specific coronary anatomies" with "diffuse lesions and/or lesions near bifurcations" — i.e., untested here.

8. **Code/licence.** "Code developed for this study will be available via online code repository" — a forward-looking promise only; no URL, repository name, or licence given anywhere in the paper. Data: "Not applicable."

9. **Stated limitations (verbatim, selected)**: "The 1D model assumes unidirectionality of the flow, however, this does not hold in the presence of a stenosis... This typically results in an underestimation of the pressure drop through the stenosis." "the estimation of trans-stenotic pressure drop... relies on a simplified geometric characterization of the focal lesion—length and minimum cross-sectional area—which may not fully represent the complexity of atherosclerotic lesions." "these differences may result from the curvature of the coronary arteries and the branching, which are not considered in the 1D representation." "We observed differences in the velocity profile proximal to the lesion comparing the 3D and the 1D–3D models. We believe these discrepancies may be the source of the WSS and shear rate errors." "Follow up studies including patient specific coronary anatomies will focus on demonstrating that this modeling approach is prepared to deal with the complexities that may arise in a patient-specific model."

10. **SUBSTITUTION JUDGEMENT: NO** — this method cannot credibly replace full 3D CFD for T6's planned 15–20-case WSS/OSI-vs-error comparison, for four compounding reasons drawn directly from the text: (a) **OSI is never computed** anywhere in this paper — T6 explicitly needs OSI, and this method provides no validated OSI pipeline or accuracy evidence for it at all, only TAWSS/TASR/TASRg/maxWSS; (b) the WSS accuracy evidence that does exist is thin and mostly qualitative — the only precise numeric error is for peak shear rate (9–10%), not TAWSS/maxWSS, and no spatial-map error metric is given, so "comparable to the 3D simulation" is not quantitatively substantiated at the level T6 would need to trust a WSS-vs-error comparison; (c) the entire validation is against the authors' own full-3D CFD of **one synthetic healthy geometry plus idealized virtual stenoses** — there is no real patient anatomy, no cohort, and no invasive ground truth, so generalization to 15–20 real, patient-specific FFR-decision cases (T6's actual cohort) is untested by this paper and is explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as future work ("as long as image segmentation is possible"); and (d) no code is currently available (only promised), so reimplementation cost/risk is unquantifiable. The 40-fold speedup is real and well-documented (Table 3), and the coupling scheme is precisely specified and reimplementable in principle, but it still requires running a full 3D CFD solver (SimVascular) on a properly segmented, meshed 3D sub-domain per case — this is not a cheap reduced-order shortcut to WSS/OSI, it is a partial-domain 3D CFD method with a 1D-tree cost saving elsewhere, and the specific quantity T6 needs (OSI) is simply absent from what this method has ever been shown to produce or validate.
