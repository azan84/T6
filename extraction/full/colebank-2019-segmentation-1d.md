## Problem & claim
- Imaging modality and domain: micro-CT (voxel dims 497×497×497, DICOM 3.0), single excised mouse lung, plus a separately measured MPA inflow flow waveform (ensemble of 20 cardiac cycles) from two male C57BL6/J mice (10–12 weeks). Domain: pulmonary hemodynamics / uncertainty quantification (UQ), motivated by pulmonary hypertension (PH) diagnosis.
- Task: Not a segmentation-algorithm paper. It is a forward+inverse UQ study: vary pre-segmentation parameters of one semi-automated segmentation pipeline applied to one image → reconstruct network geometry/connectivity → run a 1D CFD hemodynamics model → quantify how much of the downstream pressure/flow variability is attributable to (a) vessel radius/length uncertainty vs. (b) network size/connectivity uncertainty.
- Stated gap: "inherent uncertainty is present as most image segmentation software require manual specification of the image intensity thresholds (pre-segmentation parameters) between background and foreground." Prior work by van Horssen et al. [10] and Rempfler et al. [11] "quantified variability in segmented networks but did not investigate how this uncertainty affected pulsatile haemodynamics." Sankaran et al. [14] "computed 3D CFD model sensitivity to coronary stenosis diameters ... [but] did not account for possible changes in network connectivity nor for the uncertainty from the initial segmentations of the vasculature." Stated first-of-kind claim (Introduction): "to our knowledge, this is the first known investigation of the impact of uncertainties in network reconstruction on CFD simulations in the pulmonary vasculature."
- Central claim in one sentence: "Results show that variation in network connectivity is a larger contributor to haemodynamic uncertainty than vessel radius and length."

## Method
- Architecture, block level:
  1. **Segmentation** (§2.2.1): Global intensity thresholding pre-segmentation + active-contour evolution (semi-automated, ITK-SNAP [20]), always run for 2000 iterations. Two pre-segmentation parameters: θ1 (lower intensity threshold) and θ2 (smoothing). 25 realizations of (θ1, θ2) drawn from a uniform distribution, 20 ≤ θ1 ≤ 45, 3 ≤ θ2 ≤ 8, via MATLAB `rand`, all applied to the same single micro-CT image of one excised mouse lung, producing 25 "segmented networks" (SNs).
  2. **Network reconstruction** (§2.2.2): surface meshes → VTK (Paraview) → VMTK centerline/length/radius extraction → custom MATLAB recursive algorithm builds the connectivity matrix (binary-tree topology, no loops found in the region studied) and computes global network features (no. vessels, no. generations/bifurcations, total vascular volume) and morphometric indices (structured-tree scaling parameters, asymmetry/area ratios, Murray's exponent, §S.7). Radius = mean of centre 80% of per-slice VMTK estimates along each segment (to reduce ostium-region bias). Voxel-to-cm scaling via known cannula outer diameter (0.086 cm).
  3. **1D CFD model** (§2.3): cross-sectional-area-averaged mass/momentum balance (eqns 2.1–2.2) with a flat/boundary-layer velocity profile, closed by a linear thin-wall orthotropic elastic P–A law (eqn 2.4) with fixed stiffness β = Eh/r0 = 37.5 mmHg (independent of geometry). Solved with two-step Lax–Wendroff finite differences in C++, Δt = 1.3×10⁻⁵ s, Δx = 0.025 cm; vessels shorter than the spatial resolution are length-augmented to satisfy CFL. Inflow BC = measured MPA flow waveform (single cycle). Junction BCs: flow conservation + pressure continuity (eqn 2.7). Outlet BC = 3-element Windkessel per terminal vessel (eqn 2.8), R1 = 0.2 R_T, R2 = 0.8 R_T; R_T = mean pressure/mean flow; C_T from diastolic decay time constant τ = R_T C_T; terminal resistances distributed via Poiseuille's law (eqn 2.9, κᵢ = rᵢ⁴/lᵢ).
  4. **Wave intensity (WI) analysis** (§2.3.2, eqns 2.5–2.6): forward/backward WI components and pulse wave velocity (PWV), computed at the first pulmonary bifurcation.
  5. **Inverse UQ** (§2.4): a 32-vessel subset common to all 25 SNs (chosen to span the full calibre range) is standardized (z-scored) for radius and length (eqn 2.10). PDFs estimated three ways: Silverman's-rule KDE, maximum-likelihood leave-one-out cross-validation (MLCV) KDE, and logistic GP density estimation (GPstuff, Matérn ν=5/2). GP chosen as most robust ("Computations using Silverman's rule exhibit overfitting, while the maximum-likelihood cross-validation over-smooths the density relative to the GP.") Coefficient of variation (cv = σ_s/mean_s) modelled vs. mean vessel size via GP regression with input-dependent (heteroscedastic) noise; weighted-least-squares (exponential/log/sqrt/linear) tried and rejected ("unable to resolve the heteroscedastic nature of the data").
  6. **Forward UQ** (§2.5.3): inverse-CDF Monte Carlo sampling, M = 10⁴ draws, perturbing the representative network's (RN's) vessel radius/length per vessel (eqn 2.11); 1D model re-run each draw.
  7. **Three decomposition experiments** (Fig. 1, §2.5): (i) *Total variation* — run the 1D model directly on all 25 SNs. (ii) *Parameter variation* — fix connectivity/topology at the RN (the SN whose ensemble MPA pressure waveform has smallest least-squares distance to the 25-SN mean) and Monte-Carlo-perturb only radius/length (10⁴ draws). (iii) *Network variation* — fix RN's radius/length, iteratively prune terminal vessel pairs (smallest total volume V=πr²l first) while conserving total resistance, compliance and mean flow (Windkessel BCs "adjusted for each simulation"), from 219 vessels down to 3.
- Key claimed novelty: "This is the first known study to explicitly quantify the variability of 1D CFD blood flow and pressure predictions arising from uncertainty in pre-segmentation parametrization." Plus: "this study is the first to use GPs in density estimation for vascular measurements... our work is the first to consider estimated, non-parametric densities for UQ propagation, and does not require a priori distribution assumptions." Plus (Conclusion): "our study is the first to perform UQ on the dimensions and network topology of a 1D CFD model in an expansive pulmonary vascular network."
- Not a learning-based method in the ML-training sense; the closest "training" elements are the GP-regression fits (density estimation and cv-vs-size modelling).
- Inputs/outputs: input = one micro-CT DICOM (497³ voxels, 30–40 µm resolution). Output = P(x,t), Q(x,t), A(x,t) per vessel; summarized as mean/systolic/diastolic/pulse pressure, mean/max/min flow, volume, WI, PWV in MPA/LPA/RPA.

## Data
- Datasets: pre-existing micro-CT + hemodynamic data from two male C57BL6/J control mice (10–12 wks), from Vanderpool et al. 2010 [18] (imaging) and Tabima et al. 2012 [19] (hemodynamics). Segmentation study itself performed on "a single, excised mouse lung." Ethics: University of Wisconsin-Madison IACUC approval. Data released as electronic supplementary material (figshare); licence NOT REPORTED. Code released at GitHub; licence NOT REPORTED.
- Annotation protocol / number of annotators / inter-rater agreement: No human expert ground-truth annotation and no multi-observer study. One semi-automated algorithmic pipeline (ITK-SNAP active contours + global thresholding, always 2000 iterations) is re-run 25 times on the same image under 25 sampled pre-segmentation parameter pairs — a single-operator, single-algorithm, parameter-sweep design, not an inter-observer or inter-software design. No inter-rater agreement metric reported (none applicable).
- Train/val/test split: not applicable (no learned model). No external validation cohort; single-animal (single micro-CT dataset) study. Authors state: "the experimental protocol inhibited the same mouse from being used for both the haemodynamic and imaging data."

## Evaluation
- Headline geometry variability: mean 437 ± 76 vessels per network (range 185–505 across the 25 SNs, Table 1), mean ≈17 generations; total vascular volume 18.2–24.6 cm³. One clear outlier, (θ1,θ2)=(44,7.6): only 185 vessels, 12 generations, 98 terminal vessels, volume 20.44 cm³ (vs. e.g. 505 vessels/18 generations/269 terminals/24.61 cm³ for (33,5.1)).
- Headline UQ metrics: max coefficient of variation across the 32-vessel subset = 21% (radius), 49% (length). Network-variation (219→3 vessels) "introduces a discrepancy of approximately 10 mmHg in the pressure predictions of all three pulmonary arteries." Total-variation systolic-pressure s.d. "nearly six times larger than the variation of diastolic pressure."
- Baselines: no external algorithm baseline; internal comparisons only — three density-estimation methods vs. each other; four weighted-least-squares regression forms vs. GP regression; the three UQ decompositions (total/parameter/network) vs. each other.
- Statistical testing: no p-values / no segmentation-accuracy metric (no ground truth exists, stated explicitly). Uncertainty reported as ensemble mean ± s.d. (Table 2, ±2 s.d. bands in Fig. 9) and GP posterior 95% CI (Fig. 7). No classical ablation, but the three-way total/parameter/network decomposition functions as one. Authors explicitly flag Sobol-index global sensitivity analysis as the more rigorous alternative not performed, due to cost.
- Compute: not reported (no GPU; C++ PDE solver + MATLAB/GPstuff).

## Reproducibility
- Code released (GitHub, https://github.com/mjcolebank); data released (figshare, supplementary). No stated licence for either.
- Blocking reimplementation: segmentation/reconstruction/UQ methodological detail is split across electronic-supplementary-material sections §S.1–§S.7 not present in the extracted PDF text (active-contour parameters, network-reconstruction algorithm, WI derivations, Windkessel-adjustment procedure during pruning, KDE/GP methodology, morphometric-index definitions).

## Limitations
- Quoted (§4.6, Discussion): "we assume negligible tapering in each vessel, which could play a role in proximal artery dynamics." "our model assumptions ignore radius-dependent stiffness and the effects of wall viscoelasticity, which may be important in pulmonary arteries." "The length of the smallest vessels was augmented during simulations to ensure the CFL condition is not violated." "We also provided conventional mean and standard deviation calculations as familiar metrics for comparison. An alternative approach is to perform formal global sensitivity analysis. State-of-the-art methods are based on Sobol indices ... however, their computation via Monte Carlo or quasi-Monte Carlo simulations is computationally expensive." "We consider the frequently used three-element Windkessel model as the boundary condition for the 1D model, yet this model greatly simplifies the physiological resistance beyond the segmented vessels." "the experimental protocol inhibited the same mouse from being used for both the haemodynamic and imaging data. While this is a limitation for possible parameter inference, our methodology still captures variability in model predictions due to uncertainty in the vessel dimensions and network structure." "The largest vascular tree used in this study contains 500 vessels, a small fraction of the thousands of blood vessels that comprise the full pulmonary arterial system." "Our techniques study uncertainty induced by global thresholding, but could be applied when other pre-segmentation techniques are used... [it] is only one of many segmentation methods." "no experimental studies have measured pressure beyond the first few generations; hence, validation of this assumption in the pulmonary circulation is difficult."
- Not admitted but real: no ground-truth-based segmentation accuracy assessment is possible or attempted — the study measures spread across parameter choices of one pipeline, not accuracy against any reference; only a single subject/single excised lung is used for the entire UQ study; only one segmentation software/algorithm and one operator's parameter-sampling scheme are tested — no inter-software or inter-human-observer comparison at all; the "network variation" pruning is a synthetic, monotonic, smallest-volume-first truncation procedure conserving total R/C/mean-flow — not itself derived from any of the 25 real alternative segmentations, so the ~10 mmHg figure describes sensitivity to a stylized truncation experiment, not the actual observed multi-segmentation topological spread (which is never itself propagated network-by-network — see Q3 below).

## Openings
- Quoted future work: "PDF estimation methods that account for correlation between radius and length measurements should be investigated further." "A potential way to alleviate this problem [Sobol cost] is to use statistical emulation, e.g. using GPs... However, this exploration is beyond the scope of the present study and provides an interesting direction for future research." "By contrast, structured tree boundary conditions ... can provide an additional level of complexity for approximating downstream resistance and attempt to capture network structure beyond the limits of image segmentation." "Future human-based studies could incorporate non-invasive flow and imaging data from the same patient in the model." "future subject-specific models of the pulmonary vasculature would be enhanced by allowing for trifurcations and considering branching angles in the vascular tree."
- Open questions: (1) results with multiple segmentation algorithms/software or multiple human observers, vs. 25 parameter draws of one automated pipeline; (2) accuracy vs. a surrogate ground truth (e.g. corrosion cast), as opposed to variability alone; (3) generalization of the variance-decomposition/10 mmHg figure across animals, disease states (PH remodeling), or species (human translation); (4) tractable GP-emulated Sobol indices in place of mean±s.d.; (5) joint (correlated) radius–length uncertainty modelling; (6) sensitivity of the reported magnitudes to the outflow BC model choice (Windkessel vs. structured tree).

## Triage
- verdict: FULL
- reason: structurally closest prior-art paper for T6 (segmentation variation → 1D reduced-order hemodynamics), pulmonary rather than coronary domain.

## T6 decisive questions

**1. How exactly was segmentation varied?**
Not multiple human segmenters and not multiple software tools. One single operator/pipeline (ITK-SNAP active-contour evolution, always 2000 iterations) applied to one single micro-CT image of one excised mouse lung, with the two upstream *pre-segmentation* parameters (θ1 = lower intensity threshold, θ2 = smoothing) resampled 25 times from a uniform distribution: "we assume a uniform distribution for the two parameters, with 20 ≤ θ1 ≤ 45 and 3 ≤ θ2 ≤ 8, and draw 25 realizations of pre-segmentation parameter sets (θ1, θ2) ... using the rand function in MATLAB" (§2.2.1). This is a synthetic/algorithmic parameter perturbation of a single automated pipeline, not real inter-observer or inter-method variation. "By whom": one unnamed operator running the pipeline 25 times (author-team implementation, M.J.C. per the Authors' contributions statement: "performed all haemodynamic simulations, pre- and post-processing of data").

**2. Is this REAL measured inter-observer/inter-method disagreement?**
No. There is no second human reader, no second software tool, and no inter-rater agreement statistic (no Dice, no Jaccard, no Hausdorff, no ICC) anywhere in the paper — the paper explicitly states ground truth (and by extension any accuracy/agreement-to-truth metric) is unobtainable: "in vivo images are only captured up to a finite resolution, which makes ground truth rendering impossible." The only "agreement"-adjacent numbers reported are descriptive spread statistics across the 25 synthetic parameter draws, not inter-observer/inter-method agreement: number of vessels per network mean 437 ± s.d. 76 (range 185–505, Table 1); mean generations ≈17 (range 12–18); total vascular volume range 18.2–24.6 cm³; maximum coefficient of variation across the 32-vessel subset = 21% for radius, 49% for length. No volumetric-overlap (Dice/Jaccard) or boundary-distance (Hausdorff) metric is reported at all.

**3. Does the segmentation variation change the TOPOLOGY of the extracted network?**
Yes, substantially, and this is the paper's headline finding — but the topology change comes from the *same automated pipeline re-parametrized*, not from independent real segmentations disagreeing, and the specific 25-network topological variants are never individually propagated through the CFD model (see caveat below). Numbers (Table 1, §3.1): number of vessels ranges 185–505 (mean 437 ± 76 s.d.); number of generations ranges 12–18 (mean ≈17); number of terminal vessels ranges 98–269; total volume 18.2–24.6 cm³. Quote: "Results show that pre-segmentation parameters drastically influence the number of vessels in the network, while the number of generations attainable remains relatively consistent." One clear outlier, (θ1,θ2)=(44,7.6): only 185 vessels/12 generations/98 terminals — a qualitatively different, much sparser tree from the same underlying anatomy. Connectivity itself is stated as consistently a binary tree/no loops across all 25 SNs ("The resulting networks were found to have no loops within the region of interest studied here"), so what varies is vessel *count/extent* (how far the tree is resolved distally) rather than branching *pattern* (no reported cases of a bifurcation reclassified as a trifurcation, a spuriously merged/split vessel, or a genuinely different connectivity graph shape). Crucially for T6: the "network variation" experiment that produces the ~10 mmHg headline discrepancy is NOT run on the 25 real segmented topologies — it is a synthetic, monotonic pruning of the single representative network (removing smallest-volume terminal pairs, 219→3 vessels, "Windkessel boundary conditions are adjusted for each simulation" to conserve total resistance/compliance/mean flow), decoupled from any specific alternative real segmentation's topology. The paper's own "total variation" run (using the actual 25 SNs directly) is the closest analogue to what T6 wants, and it reports the smallest of the three variance bands (Table 2: total variation MPA systolic 35.35±1.63 vs. parameter-only 35.37±1.03 vs. network[pruning]-only 31.70±2.07) — i.e., the study's synthetic connectivity-pruning experiment shows a *larger* pressure effect than the real 25-segmentation ensemble spread itself, which is a nuance a careful review would need to unpack. There is no missed-side-branch / topological misclassification error type as T6 defines it (a specific vessel dropped or added relative to a reference anatomy); rather it is progressive truncation of distal resolution.

**4. What is the 1D/reduced-order model, and what outputs are compared?**
Cross-sectional-area-averaged 1D Navier–Stokes-type equations (mass + momentum, eqns 2.1–2.2) closed with a linear orthotropic thin-wall elastic P–A law (eqn 2.4, fixed β=37.5 mmHg), solved via 2-step Lax–Wendroff FD (Δt=1.3×10⁻⁵ s, Δx=0.025 cm) in C++. Outputs compared: mean/systolic/diastolic/pulse pressure and max/min flow and total volume in MPA/LPA/RPA (Table 2); wave-intensity (WI) forward/backward components at the first pulmonary bifurcation (Fig. 10); pulse wave velocity (PWV). Every reported segmentation-attributable numeric difference:
- Total variation (25 SNs): MPA mean 20.36±0.78 mmHg, systolic 35.35±1.63, diastolic 10.02±0.27, pulse 25.33±1.39; LPA mean 19.66±0.79, systolic 33.46±1.67; RPA mean 19.52±0.78, systolic 32.83±1.60.
- Parameter variation (radius/length MC only, fixed connectivity): MPA mean 20.38±0.54, systolic 35.37±1.03, diastolic 10.04±0.23, pulse 25.33±0.82 — narrower s.d. than total.
- Network variation (pruning only, fixed radius/length): MPA mean 18.29±0.84, systolic 31.70±2.07, diastolic 9.08±0.18, pulse 22.63±1.91 — wider s.d. for systolic/pulse than parameter variation, and lower absolute mean pressure (network truncation systematically lowers predicted pressure).
- "Overall, reducing the number of vessels from 219 in the largest network to 3 in the smallest network introduces a discrepancy of approximately 10 mmHg in the pressure predictions of all three pulmonary arteries."
- "The standard deviation of the pulse pressure for the parameter variation only accounted for approximately 30% of the total variation value."
- PWV: total 4.83±0.0054 m/s; parameter 4.83±0.0037 m/s; network 4.84±0.0054 m/s ("a minimal relative difference").
- "changes in network topology due to segmentation induced a variation in systolic pressure that was nearly six times larger than the variation of diastolic pressure."

**5. Boundary conditions: how set, and re-calibrated per segmentation?**
BCs are set (not fitted to data via optimization/inverse parameter estimation) directly from each network's own geometry and physiology on every run: inflow = the same fixed measured MPA flow waveform for all runs (never varies); Windkessel R1=0.2R_T, R2=0.8R_T with R_T = mean pressure/mean flow (Poiseuille-based a priori distribution across terminals, eqn 2.9) and C_T from the diastolic time constant τ=R_T C_T (§2.3.4). This is a deterministic, closed-form BC-assignment rule applied fresh to each network's own vessel dimensions — not an optimization-based re-tuning against a validation target. Quote confirming BCs are geometry-dependent and recomputed per network: "parameters specifying the vessel radius, length and Windkessel outflow boundary conditions (R1, R2, CT) depend on the network structure." For the network-pruning experiment specifically, the paper states BCs are explicitly re-adjusted after each pruning step to conserve global physiological targets: "we reduce the network by starting at the smallest branches and moving towards the proximal vasculature while ensuring that Windkessel boundary conditions are adjusted for each simulation" (§3.3), and in Methods: "Total resistance, total compliance and total mean flow in the network are conserved during this process" (§2.5.4). This IS a form of re-calibration after a geometry change (T6 contribution (d) territory) — but it re-derives BCs analytically from Poiseuille's law and conservation constraints, not by fitting to reproduce a validation waveform/target via an inverse/optimization procedure, and there is no MCMC/optimization-based parameter-inference step anywhere in this paper (the companion paper Qureshi et al. 2019 [3], cited throughout, is where such inverse parameter estimation is performed; this paper explicitly does not do parameter inference: "this is a limitation for possible parameter inference, our methodology still captures variability in model predictions due to uncertainty in the vessel dimensions and network structure"). There is NO statement anywhere that this BC re-adjustment "absorbs," "masks," "compensates for," or hides the geometric differences so that predictions match a validation target despite wrong anatomy — on the contrary, the paper's entire point is that this BC-conserving re-adjustment still leaves a visible ~10 mmHg pressure discrepancy: the re-derivation of BCs does NOT make the pruned networks' predictions converge to the un-pruned network's predictions. The paper never poses or tests the specific question T6 asks (does re-tuning make the model look "validated" despite wrong anatomy) — it only shows that even under a conservation-constrained BC rule, geometry differences still produce a visible pressure effect. Plainly: **re-tuning-as-masking is NOT tested or discussed in this paper.**

**6. Is any binary decision/threshold outcome evaluated?**
No. Only continuous differences (mmHg, cm³ s⁻¹, cm³, m/s) are reported throughout — pressures, flows, volumes, WI, PWV. There is no reclassification analysis, no FFR-style dichotomous threshold, no case-by-case decision-flip count, and no ROC/AUC-type evaluation anywhere in the paper. The paper does gesture qualitatively at clinical relevance of continuous metrics ("All four of these pressure metrics are typically used in diagnostic tools of diseases such as PH," and mean PA pressure ≥25 mmHg is quoted in the Introduction as the clinical PH diagnostic threshold), but it never operationalizes a binary decision outcome or evaluates how many of the 25 (or 10⁴, or pruned) networks would cross that or any other threshold.

**7. Species/domain and generalization claims.**
Species/domain: mouse pulmonary arterial circulation exclusively (single excised lung, micro-CT, C57BL6/J mice) — no human data anywhere in the study. Generalization statements are limited and qualified, not a claim of direct transfer to human coronary FFR: "The animal dataset used here [18,19] serves as a preliminary step in understanding disease progression and has potential for extrapolation to human PH" (end of Introduction) and "Future human-based studies could incorporate non-invasive flow and imaging data from the same patient in the model" (§4.6). No mention of coronary arteries, coronary FFR, or FFR≤0.80 anywhere in the paper; the only FFR-related citation is to Sankaran et al. [14], a 3D coronary-stenosis sensitivity study, discussed purely as related prior work on segmentation-uncertainty sensitivity, not as a target domain for this paper's own findings. The paper explicitly flags a physiological reason the pulmonary result may NOT transfer to systemic/coronary circulation: "In contrast with the systemic circulation, the pulmonary vasculature is more compliant, branches more rapidly and operates at a much lower mean pressure, indicating that results from the systemic circulation may not be valid for comparison" (Introduction) — an argument that, read in reverse, is exactly the kind of statement a reviewer could invoke to argue the pulmonary-to-coronary direction is likewise non-trivial and that T6's human coronary FFR study is not "incremental" over this one.

**8. Own stated limitations and future work (verbatim), see "Limitations" and "Openings" sections above** for the full quoted set; key ones bearing directly on T6: tapering ignored ("we assume negligible tapering in each vessel"); radius-dependent stiffness/viscoelasticity ignored; Windkessel BC "greatly simplifies the physiological resistance beyond the segmented vessels," with structured-tree BCs proposed as future work; Sobol-index global sensitivity analysis flagged as more rigorous but not performed; single-mouse/single-segmentation-pipeline scope explicitly limits generalizability (not stated as a limitation in those words but evident from design); "future subject-specific models of the pulmonary vasculature would be enhanced by allowing for trifurcations and considering branching angles."

**9. Decisive judgement on T6 contributions (a)–(d).**

- **(a) Segmentation-error magnitudes from REAL measured inter-observer disagreement:** NOT COVERED. This paper's error magnitudes come from resampling two pre-segmentation *algorithm parameters* (θ1, θ2) of one automated pipeline run by (implicitly) one operator on one image — there is no second human segmenter, no second software tool, and no inter-observer/inter-method agreement statistic (no Dice, no ICC, no volume/diameter delta between independent segmenters) anywhere in the paper. The paper itself distinguishes its design from "true" inter-observer/inter-algorithm comparison work (Rempfler et al. [11]) and explicitly says ground truth (and hence any accuracy-based or true inter-rater-based error magnitude) is unobtainable for its imaging modality.
- **(b) DISTINCT error types injected separately (stenosis-length, eccentric lumen, missed side-branch/topological, taper):** NOT COVERED. The paper's decomposition is topologically coarse (parameter [radius/length] vs. network [size/connectivity via monotonic smallest-volume pruning]) and does not inject or isolate stenosis-length error, eccentric-lumen error, a missed-side-branch error, or a taper error as distinct, separately-controlled perturbation types. Tapering is explicitly excluded from the model altogether ("we assume negligible tapering in each vessel"), and the "network variation" pruning is a single monotonic truncation procedure, not a set of qualitatively distinct topological error modes (e.g., a specific side-branch dropped vs. a genuinely misconnected bifurcation).
- **(c) RANKING error types by effect on a BINARY clinical decision (FFR≤0.80/reclassification):** NOT COVERED. No binary decision or threshold-crossing/reclassification analysis of any kind is performed; all outcomes are continuous (mmHg, flow, volume, WI, PWV). No FFR or FFR-like threshold appears anywhere in the paper.
- **(d) Testing whether RE-TUNING boundary conditions after a geometry change ABSORBS/MASKS the error:** PARTIALLY COVERED, and this is the axis of greatest overlap risk. The paper does re-derive Windkessel BCs after every geometry change (each of the 25 SNs, and after every pruning step in the network-variation study), explicitly stating BCs "are adjusted for each simulation" to conserve total resistance/compliance/mean flow. However: (i) this re-derivation is analytic/Poiseuille-and-conservation-based, not an inverse/optimization fit of BCs to reproduce a validation target waveform, so it is a different sense of "re-tuning" than T6's likely design (which — per context — probably involves refitting BCs against a validation/target dataset after a geometry perturbation); (ii) the paper never asks or answers whether this re-adjustment *masks* the geometric error (makes the model look validated despite wrong anatomy) — instead it reports that even after BC re-adjustment, the pruned/perturbed networks still produce a visible ~10 mmHg pressure discrepancy, i.e., the paper's own data argue AGAINST masking, but it never frames or discusses the question in masking/absorption terms at all, so this remains an open framing T6 can genuinely claim as novel.

**One-paragraph verdict on novelty threat.** This paper is the closest methodological precedent for T6 in one specific respect — it is the only work found so far that propagates real segmentation-parameter variation through a 1D/reduced-order hemodynamic model and explicitly decomposes the resulting uncertainty into a "vessel-geometry" component versus a "network-connectivity/size" component, concluding (as T6 would likely also want to argue) that connectivity/topology dominates over simple geometric parameter noise — a reviewer could reasonably write "the qualitative finding that topological/connectivity variation matters more than radius/length noise for reduced-order hemodynamic predictions was already shown in the pulmonary circulation (Colebank et al. 2019); the coronary case is incremental." That said, the threat is narrow and largely defensible on four grounds this note has now nailed down with quotes: (1) the error source here is synthetic algorithm-parameter resampling of ONE pipeline, not real measured inter-observer/inter-method disagreement — T6's contribution (a) is untouched; (2) there is no injection of qualitatively distinct, clinically-motivated error types (stenosis-length, eccentric lumen, missed side-branch, taper) — the "network variation" here is a single monotonic distal-truncation procedure, not a taxonomy of topological error modes, so T6's contribution (b) is untouched; (3) there is no binary/reclassification outcome or FFR-style threshold anywhere in the paper — T6's contribution (c), which is arguably its most clinically legible and defensible novelty, is completely absent here; (4) although BCs are recomputed after geometry changes, this is a closed-form conservation rule, not an inverse-fit re-tuning against a validation target, and the paper never frames, tests, or discusses whether such re-tuning could mask/absorb geometric error and produce a falsely "validated" but anatomically wrong model — T6's contribution (d), in its masking/absorption framing, is at most gestured at, not addressed. On balance: this paper threatens T6 mainly on the general framing axis ("segmentation-uncertainty-into-reduced-order-model propagation, topology matters most" is not new), and a rigorous reviewer should be pre-empted by explicitly citing and distinguishing this paper on exactly these four axes in the manuscript's related-work section; it does not threaten the decisive/binary-outcome framing (c) or the BC-masking framing (d), which remain open.
