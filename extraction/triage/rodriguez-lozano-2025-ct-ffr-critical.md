# rodriguez-lozano-2025-ct-ffr-critical

## Bibliographic
- Title: CT derived fractional flow reserve: Part 2 – Critical appraisal of the literature
- First author / authors (first 3 + et al.): Patricia F. Rodriguez-Lozano, Anam Waheed, Sotirios Evangelou et al.
- Year: 2025
- Venue: Journal of Cardiovascular Computed Tomography
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jcct.2025.05.241

## One-line claim
Comprehensive critical review of CT-FFR diagnostic accuracy, clinical utility, and limitations; addresses pitfalls and proposes solutions for implementation in clinical practice.

## T6 targeted questions
- **Q-A geometry perturbation**: NOT REPORTED directly. Discusses lesion characteristics (length, plaque burden, remodeling index, stenosis degree) but no measurement of segmentation/observer uncertainty.
- **Q-B decision flip**: Yes; FFR ≤0.80 threshold is discussed extensively. Documents risk continuum: each 0.10-unit reduction in CT-FFR associated with greater MACE risk (RR 1.67, 95% CI 1.47–1.87).
- **Q-C BC tuning**: NOT REPORTED. Boundary condition strategies mentioned for CFD-based methods but no discussion of re-tuning after geometry change or compensation mechanisms.
- **Q-D fidelity / quantity**: Discusses multiple CT-FFR approaches (CFD-based HeartFlow, Siemens; AI/ML methods) and emerging WSS/plaque assessment; no dedicated 3D CFD coronary validation presented.
- **Q-E data**: Meta-analysis and multi-trial review; N=5,689 from five observational studies/registries with 1-5 year follow-up. Mixed public/private cohorts. Invasive FFR reference standard used.
- **Q-F meshing**: Briefly mentions segmentation errors and image quality issues affecting CT-FFR accuracy; notes exclusion of 25% of cases in PACIFIC-1 due to suboptimal image quality. Manual segmentation requirements noted as limitation.

## Novelty bearing on T6
- bucket: SUPPORT
- one-line reason: Comprehensive review validating FFR-based decision-making and documenting CT-FFR performance; supports T6 premise that functional assessment guides revascularization but does not address geometric error or BC compensation.
- verdict: FULL
- revisit-if: Any new evidence on segmentation-induced FFR bias or boundary condition sensitivity in intermediate stenosis.
