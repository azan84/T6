## Review Body

**Strongest counter-argument.** The paper calls its central phenomenon "concealment": tuning removes a segmentation error from the perfusion check but not from the FFR. The manuscript's own higher-fidelity evidence points the other way. In the 3D case, the clean territory flows were prescribed at the outlets of the missed-branch geometry, and the error in FFR disappeared (ΔFFR −0.0007; 0.892 in both geometries). The reduced-order twin under the same prescribed flows gave −0.0005. So when a model is genuinely matched to territory perfusion, the missed-branch error is removed from the FFR as well as from the check, and nothing is concealed. The material error in Protocol C survives for two reasons. First, the tuning is deliberately restricted to one global scaling of the bed constant, which cannot reproduce the territory split. Second, the "pass" threshold tolerates up to 10% RMS flow mismatch, enough to move the pressure drop across a severe lesion by more than 0.05. On this reading, concealment is an artefact of a tuning that is under-parameterised and only loosely checked, not a property of perfusion tuning. Clinical pipelines that personalise resistances per territory would be expected to behave like the 3D case, not like Protocol C. Three further points support this rival account. In the leaky bed, untuned Protocol A models pass while wrong more often than tuned ones (20% against 8%). At 0.7 times the demand, the leaky-bed pass-while-wrong rate (3%) equals the correct-anatomy floor (3.1–3.9%). And the residual that defines "passing" is computed against the same noise-free targets used for fitting. The topological-versus-caliber ranking is equally exposed. It compares the worst topological error the authors could choose (the largest distal side branch, or a break 25 mm beyond the lesion) with caliber errors capped at inter-observer agreement. The ratio of 3 to 6 is therefore a property of the chosen magnitudes, not of the error classes.

**Logic chain, claim by claim.** (i) Abstract: "With fixed boundary conditions, the branching (topological) errors changed the decision in 32–33% of models" is supported by Table I, but only for the chosen magnitudes and a cohort enriched near 0.80 by stratified sampling. (ii) Abstract and Discussion say "Re-derived or tuned boundary conditions reduced topological decision changes to 5–20%". This is supported, but the Table I denominators differ between protocols (T1: A n = 77, C n = 44 in the discrete bed), so the range mixes subsets. (iii) The concealment claim is supported as an existence result inside the reduced-order model with one-parameter tuning. It is not supported as a statement about perfusion-matched models in general (C1, M2, M5). (iv) "A three-dimensional case reproduced this concealment" (Conclusion) is contradicted by the reported 3D numbers (C1). (v) The practical advice to "treat diameter errors of inter-observer size as a minor risk" ignores the paper's own taper result under tuning (M4).

**Cherry-picking and overgeneralisation (MINOR, recorded here because only CRITICAL and MAJOR take tables).** The Conclusion quotes the most extreme cell ("from 43% of models with fixed boundary conditions to 5% with re-derived ones for a vessel break in the leaky bed"), not the cross-bed range. In the discrete bed, the pooled caliber flip rate under Protocol A is 10% (7–15%), and its lower bound exceeds the 5.0–6.5% repeat-measurement floor. That sits uneasily with "close to the variability of repeat invasive measurement" in the Abstract (confidence 4, read directly from Table I and Results III-A). Protocol C was undefined for most RCA topological instances. The tuned-model claims therefore describe the left coronary tree, yet the Abstract and Conclusion carry no qualifier (confidence 4). Pre-specification is asserted ("the cohort list hashed"), but no registry, hash or timestamp is disclosed and the code is available only on request. The pre-specification claim therefore cannot be checked by a reader (confidence 3; adjacent to the methodology seat's remit). The 3D Protocol C clean-lesion FFR (0.892) differs from the 3D Protocol A value (0.870) in the same geometry. The prescribed "clean" flows are therefore not the 3D clean model's own flows, which weakens the case's role as a paired 3D test of Protocol C (confidence 3, from Results III-D and Fig. 5).

**What survives.** The ablation design is clean. Its admission that tuning reduced flips, against the authors' expectation, is to their credit. The demonstration that a pre-tuning perfusion mismatch flags a missing branch (Protocol B passes while wrong in only 1–5%) is a useful and defensible result. Reframed around "record the mismatch before tuning", and with the concealment claim confined to global-scaling tuning, the paper's core contribution holds.
