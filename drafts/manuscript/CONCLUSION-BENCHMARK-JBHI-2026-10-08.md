## 2. Fu et al., 2021

**Full Citation:**  
Fu, W., Sharma, S., Abadi, E., Iliopoulos, A.-S., Wang, Q., Lo, J.Y., Sun, X., Segars, W.P., & Samei, E. (2021). iPhantom: A framework for automated creation of individualized computational phantoms and its application to CT organ dosimetry. *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics*, 25(8), 3061–3072.

**DOI:** 10.1109/JBHI.2021.3063080

**Source URL:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9366043

**Publication Year:** 2021

**CONCLUSION (Verbatim):**

"Computational human phantoms (CHPs) are essential for personalized clinical investigations and population-based simulation studies. However, their utility and generalization have been limited by current approaches in creating CHPs using manual segmentation. In this study, we proposed a novel framework, *i*Phantom, for automated and accurate creation of patient-specific CHPs from patient medical images. We showed that the framework precisely localized a wide range of organs, including low contrast organs, in CT images. Specifically, we presented an integrated framework built on fusing patient-specific automated learning-based segmentation with anatomical templates through template matching and diffeomorphic deformation. This framework was applied to patient-specific organ dosimetry, yielding a high accuracy (< 10% organ dose error) across radiosensitive organs. The framework demonstrates modular design enabling future optimization for specialized applications, particularly addressing challenges with difficult-to-segment organs and limited training data scenarios."

**CONCLUSION (Verbatim - Partial):**

"This model combines the U-Mamba architecture with an uncertainty-aware loss function and the SAM optimizer, resulting in a substantial enhancement. ## 8. Uncertainty Propagation for Echocardiography Clinical Metric Estimation via Contour Sampling, 2025

**Full Citation:**  
Judge, T., Bernard, O., Cho Kim, W.-J., Gomez, A., Beqiri, A., Chartsias, A., & Jodoin, P.-M. (2025). Uncertainty propagation for echocardiography clinical metric estimation via contour sampling. *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*, [Volume X], [Page range].

**DOI:** arXiv 2502.12713

**arXiv ID:** 2502.12713v1

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12713

**Publication Year:** 2025

**CONCLUSION (Verbatim):**

"In this work, we presented a novel framework for uncertainty propagation in the estimation of clinical metrics from segmentations derived from echocardiographic images. This framework provides an intelligible uncertainty estimation that can combine both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. Although this adaptable framework accommodates any uncertainty method capable of generating samples, our findings highlight that a contour-based approach to aleatoric uncertainty, combined with shape uncertainty modeling, delivers optimal results in clinical metric calibration. This approach is further improved by integrating temporal consistency in the sampling process. One limitation of our work is the incompatibility between the variability required to achieve clinical metric calibration and pixel-wise uncertainty calibration. The increased variability in sample generation needed for modeling clinical uncertainty leads to under-confident pixel-wise estimations. CASUS and SSN exhibit better calibration on clinical metrics but show under-confidence in pixel-wise calibration. Alternative methods for generating uncertainty maps from sample sets may further enhance calibration."

**Word Count:** 177 | **Sentence Count:** 8 | **Structure:** Problem/contribution → framework capabilities (dual uncertainty types) → methodological finding (contour approach superior) → key innovation (temporal consistency) → limitations identified (variability trade-off) → under-confidence in pixel-wise → alternative improvements. Contribution explicit ("presented a novel framework"). Trade-offs acknowledged. No future work section, but mentions alternative improvements needed. Note: This is IEEE TMI, not JBHI.

## Key Structural Patterns Observed

**Typical JBHI Conclusion Structure:**
1. **Restatement of contribution** (explicit "we show/present")
2. **Key findings** (often with specific numbers: DSC scores, percentages, error rates, inter-patient variation)
3. **Clinical implication or practical benefit** (radiation reduction, cost, speed, diagnostic precision, patient outcome)
4. **Generalization/validation scope** (across domains, datasets, modalities)
5. **Future work or limitations** (variable: some conclude with future directions, others with clinical integration pathways)

**Numeric Results:**
- Dice/DSC scores common (87–93%)
- Error rates explicit (< 10%)
- Comparative improvements stated (surpass nnUNet, etc.)
- Large variation ranges noted (337–393%)

**Contribution Language:**
- "We present/show/demonstrate"
- "Framework/method for X"
- "Enables/facilitates" (benefit-focused language)

**User/Practical Benefits Named:**
- Radiation exposure reduction
- Cost savings
- Workflow streamlining
- Diagnostic precision
- Patient outcome improvement
- Clinical integration feasibility

**Future Work Mention:**
- ~40–60% of samples include future directions
- Often tied to clinical validation, broader datasets, or clinical deployment
- Some frame as limitations to address

## 9. UU-Mamba: Uncertainty-aware U-Mamba for Cardiac Image Segmentation, 2024

**Full Citation:**  
Tsai, T.Y., Lin, L., Hu, S., Chang, M.-C., Zhu, H., & Wang, X. (2024). UU-Mamba: Uncertainty-aware U-Mamba for cardiac image segmentation. *arXiv preprint* 2405.17496v4.

**arXiv ID:** 2405.17496v4

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17496

**Publication Year:** 2024

**CONCLUSION (Verbatim):**

"We introduce a new model, UU-Mamba, designed for 3D heart segmentation in cardiac MRI images. This model integrates the U-Mamba architecture with an uncertainty-aware loss function and the SAM optimizer. Our method improves biological image segmentation by improving generalization and boundary accuracy. The uncertainty-aware loss combines region-based, distribution-based, and pixel-based losses to improve segmentation performance by harmonizing tasks and prioritizing confident predictions. Meanwhile, the SAM optimizer guides the model toward flat minima in the loss landscape, enhancing resilience and reducing overfitting, ultimately resulting in more accurate segmentation. Comparative analysis against five leading models demonstrates the superiority of UU-Mamba, achieving a DSC of 92.787%, signifying both accuracy and robustness in segmentation performance. Future work will encompass the exploration of additional data augmentation techniques, the investigation of alternative uncertainty modeling methods, and the validation of the model on larger and more diverse datasets. Our overarching aim is to advance automated medical imaging by continuously refining and broadening the UU-Mamba approach."

**Word Count:** 183 | **Sentence Count:** 8 | **Structure:** Model introduction → architecture components (U-Mamba, uncertainty loss, SAM) → method advantages (generalization, boundary) → loss function details (region/distribution/pixel-based) → optimizer mechanism (flat minima, resilience) → comparative results (DSC 92.787%, superiority statement) → future work (data augmentation, uncertainty modeling, broader datasets). Numeric results included (92.787% DSC). Contribution explicit ("introduce a new model"). Future work section included. Note: arXiv preprint, not yet in peer-reviewed JBHI.

## Recommended JBHI Conclusion Structure for Paper 6 (T6)

Based on the 6 confirmed JBHI papers, the typical high-impact conclusion:

1. **Opening statement** (~20 words): Restate contribution + main finding
2. **Method + validation scope** (~40–60 words): What was done, across which domains/datasets
3. **Key numeric results** (~20–40 words): DSC, error rate, or variability range
4. **Clinical implication** (~30–50 words): Benefit to patient, clinician, or workflow
5. **Clinical integration pathway** (~20–40 words): Path to deployment or next validation step
6. **Future work** (~20–40 words): One direction (not multiple), tied to clinical adoption

**Total target range: 150–220 words; 5–8 sentences**
