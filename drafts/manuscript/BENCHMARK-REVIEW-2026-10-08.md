## 3. Strengths (with evidence)

1. **Design rigour exceeds every paper in the set.** Paired within-instance contrasts, interval estimates for every proportion, multiplicity control, two independent noise floors and a stated rule that a finding must replicate in both bed structures (D-§II-F). No benchmark paper has any of these; B3 and B6 have no inferential statistics at all.
2. **Transparency of deviations.** Supplement S1 lists 13 departures from the plan, including analyses "not run" and a mesh acceptance criterion that "was not met". This is rare in JBHI and will read as credible to a careful reviewer.
3. **Honest reporting of an unexpected result** (tuning reduced, not preserved, flips; D-§III-B) and of the 3D radius discrepancy (0.761 vs 0.870; D-§III-D).
4. **Plain-language definitions** of every technical term, with sentence lengths well below B1/B5/B6; a non-specialist informatics reviewer can follow the paper.
5. **No promotional language**; the Conclusion restates findings only, as the 2026-10-03 plan required.
6. **Table II** is a complete, self-contained results table with definitions, n, CIs and the noise floor in the footnote. It is better than any table in B1–B6.
7. **Reconciliation of prior conflicting findings** (Gamage 2022 vs Gosling 2020) by protocol is a genuine contribution to the literature and is stated in one paragraph without criticising either study (D-§IV-B).

## 6. Overall verdict

On the science the draft is **better than the JBHI benchmark median**: its controlled design, interval reporting, paired tests with multiplicity control, two noise floors, replication rule across bed structures, and public deviations list have no counterpart in any of the six published papers read, including the 2026 FSI paper that is the closest in kind (four patients, no statistics, resistances tuned to a 3% flow-split tolerance without sensitivity). Clarity and language are also above the median: definitions are plain, sentences are 20–25 words against 30–35 in three benchmarks, and there is no promotional phrasing.

The draft is **weaker than the median on what a JBHI reviewer sees first**: it has the fewest figures in the set and none that shows an image or a segmentation; its 8 pages sit at the journal's 10th percentile for length; it cites no JBHI paper and only 29 references; it offers code on request rather than a repository; and it has no contact with real segmentation error even though the dataset it uses carries a second annotation. The fit-to-scope risk is real (no informatics component) but is manageable through the SI call and by adopting the credibility vocabulary the journal has published (Viceconti 2025).

Fixes 2, 3, 5, 7–11, 13 and 14 are text-only or plotting-only and fit within 8 pages by removing Fig. 1 and the Results-A bookkeeping. Fix 1 (real inter-observer topology count) is the single change most likely to move a reviewer from "interesting simulation" to "evidence", and it is feasible if the second-annotator centrelines are in the download; fix 4/5 (a figure of the error types on a real tree, and a legible Fig. 5) is the second. With fixes 1–6 done the paper would be on par or better on every parameter except length and real-data validation, both of which are declared limitations. Without them, expect a major revision centred on validation, figures and literature rather than on the science.

**Bottom line:** submit after fixes 1–6 (if fix 1 proves infeasible, do its Limitations sentence); the remaining fixes are an afternoon's work and should also be done.
