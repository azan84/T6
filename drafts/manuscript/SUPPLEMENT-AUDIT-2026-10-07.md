## Errors

**1. (Error) S1 item 1, lines 24-26: the GEE statement is wrong on both the outcome and the cause.**
Text: "A patient-clustered generalised estimating equation fitted as a check did not converge, because several error-type and protocol cells are identical (T3 under Protocols A and B) or nearly separated."
Evidence: `P1_gee_discrete.csv` holds finite estimates for every term and the re-fit reports `converged = True`; the error-type and protocol coefficients (T2 +0.59, T3 -1.94, T4 -1.17, B -1.61, C -0.71) have the same signs as the Bayesian model. The one degenerate term is the 0.90-0.95 band (-3.1e139, SE 1.7e-16): that band has 0 flips in 102 rows, i.e. complete separation on a band indicator. `P1_gee_leaky.csv` is all NaN and the re-fit reports `converged = False`; in the leaky bed the 0.85-0.90 and 0.90-0.95 bands have 0 flips in 538 rows. The identical T3 cells do not stop the fit: the discrete GEE returns T3 x B = +1.6102, exactly cancelling B = -1.6102.
Fix: "A patient-clustered generalised estimating equation fitted as a check gave estimates in the discrete bed with the same sign as the Bayesian model for every error-type and protocol term, but with a degenerate coefficient for the 0.90--0.95 band, in which no model flipped. In the leaky bed, where no model in the two upper bands flipped, it did not converge. The main-text inferences rest on the paired McNemar, sign and Wilcoxon tests."

**4. (Error) S3, lines 79-80: the model summary generalises reference-level terms to "both beds".**
Text: "vessel breaks (T2) carry higher odds of a flip than missed branches (T1), the calibre errors (T3, T4) lower odds, and Protocols B and C lower odds than Protocol A in both beds."

In the leaky bed the T2 x B (-1.89) and T2 x C (-2.00) interactions cancel the T2 main effect (+1.81), so under re-derived or tuned boundary conditions the two topological errors have similar odds, as Table II shows (5% vs 5% under B, 8% vs 8% under C). Fix: "At the reference levels the model agrees with the paired tests: under fixed boundary conditions a vessel break carries higher odds of a flip than a missed branch and the calibre errors lower odds, and for the missed branch Protocols B and C carry lower odds than Protocol A, in both beds. In the leaky bed the T2 $\times$ B and T2 $\times$ C interactions offset the T2 main effect, so under re-derived or tuned boundary conditions the two topological errors have similar odds, as in Table II of the main text."

## Summary

Every table cell in the supplement matches the data, and the headline 3D numbers, noise-floor constants and convergence counts are correct. Six statements in the prose are wrong against the data or the records: the GEE did not fail for the stated reason and did converge in the discrete bed (1); the ablation did not solve every instance in the discrete bed (2); one 3D mesh variant did not meet the strict convergence rule (3); the mixed-model summary and the demand-sensitivity "every contrast unchanged" claims are overstated, the latter also in main.tex line 369 (4, 5); and the "no bed left" exclusion occurs in both beds (6). The main structural gap is that the T1 and T2 applicability exclusions (20 + 32 missed-branch and 1 + 1 vessel-break instances) are absent from Table S1, so the 77/118 and 96/149 denominators are explained nowhere (14), and the Departures list omits the unfitted ΔFFR mixed model, the unrun secondary and pre-seen-instance sensitivities, the pooled bed model, the unplanned GEE and the plan's extra uncertainty floors (15). The "code fixed before the run" sentence should be narrowed to the ablation code (7). The remaining items are footnotes (8, 9, 13, 17), one gate description (11), an empty subsection and float placement (16), a stale-PDF build guard (20), and wording (19, 21-26).
