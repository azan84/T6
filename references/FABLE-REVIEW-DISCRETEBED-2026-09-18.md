## 0. One fact that changes the framing: the table in the request was not produced by the committed code

Reproducing the request's table with `Tree(bed="discrete")` as committed (top-down share) gives **878_left 0.751,
844_left 0.883, 3_left 0.688** — not 0.829 / 0.907 / 0.800. Those three numbers coincide exactly with the **leaf r_ref³**

three single-outlet trees (878_right, 686_right, 80_right) are scheme-independent, which is why they match either way.
So the "observed consequence" was measured under the scheme the memo rejects, and the committed scheme is worse on
every one of the multi-outlet trees. Whatever is decided below, the decision memo must be re-run against the code
that implements it.

## 1. Decision (c), top-down Murray share — **REJECT the justification; REPLACE with leaf r_ref^2.66 (or r³)**

**The premise is factually correct on this data.** At the 0.75 mm cut the outlets are all at the cut
(`cohort140_leaf.csv` [r_leaf]: median 0.752 mm, p90 0.773, p95 0.834; 92 % of leaves < 0.80 mm; per-tree
max/min leaf r³ ratio median **1.02**, p90 1.54; per-tree CV of leaf r³ median **0.011**). Leaf r³ is, as the memo
says, twig-counting. Share does spread the weights (per-tree max/min share ratio median 2.34, p90 3.57;
main-vessel leaves get mean share 0.40 of inlet demand vs 0.23 under leaf r³).

**The conclusion does not follow: the split does not matter where the paper measures.** All six schemes deliver the
same excess flow to the lesion and measurement nodes relative to the leaky reference
(`cohort140_slot.csv`, T = 0.75, [Qh_les / Qh_les_leaky] and [Qh_meas / Qh_meas_leaky], healthy network, n = 120
host slots): at the lesion node ×1.19 (share) vs ×1.17 (leaf3, leaf266, origin) vs ×1.19 (apport); at the
measurement node ×1.49 vs ×1.51 vs ×1.51 vs ×1.50, IQR ×1.29–×2.0–2.2 for all. The mean |log ratio| at the lesion
node is 0.286 (share), 0.284 (leaf3), 0.278 (apport), 0.275 (origin) — indistinguishable. The 65 %DS FFR at the
measurement node is offset from leaky by −0.048 (share) vs −0.046 (leaf3) vs −0.041 (origin) [les65_meas −
les65_meas_leaky]; decision disagreement with leaky at 65 %DS: 32 / 34 / 27 of 120. The excess flow — hence the
systematic offset — comes from the **structure** (no wall leak, so the along-taper Murray flow reaches the tips),
not from how the tips share it. The memo's objection to twig-counting is real but cures nothing measurable.

**On the tree-level statistic share is the worse scheme.** Min-FFR over resolved main vessels vs leaky
(`cohort140_tree.csv` [minffr_0.75_*] − [leaky_minffr], n = 140): share mean −0.056, SD 0.077, min −0.433,
FFR ≤ 0.80 disagreement 17/140, r = 0.59; leaf3 −0.044, SD 0.054, min −0.267, disagreement 13, r = 0.71;
leaf266 same as leaf3 with disagreement 11; apport −0.053 / 0.067 / −0.374 / 15; origin −0.048 / 0.062 / −0.333 / 14;
length −0.061 / 0.084 / −0.462 / 17. The share scheme's large-offset tail is produced by exactly the case the memo
wanted to fix: a main-vessel leaf inherits the whole Murray share of its trunk, all of it is pushed through a
~0.75 mm tip past a distal native narrowing (878_left LAD: 0.73 mL/s through the distal LAD under share vs 0.14 under
leaky, `segment_table`), which is the 3_left/878_left collapse.

**Which is most defensible.** Leaf r^2.66 or r³ — the outlet-diameter allocation of Taylor 2013 / Sankaran 2012
[UNVERIFIED, from memory] and of Mao 2025 Eq. 7 (Murray on outlet cap areas, already cited in CFD-ARM-SPEC §0) —
for three reasons that do not depend on the numbers: (i) it is what the 3D arm will impose (R_i ∝ 1/r_i^n on the cap),
so 0D-discrete and 3D-discrete share their BCs exactly; (ii) it is the leaky model's own subtree total — by
telescoping, Σ_{subtree(v)} w_leaky = r_ref(v)³ — so "Murray applied to the truncated branch" *is* leaf r³, no new
construct needed; (iii) the share scheme has no precedent I can point to in the corpus (bifurcation-wise Murray split
resembles van der Giessen 2011 [UNVERIFIED]) and a reviewer will ask why an invented allocation was preferred to the
standard one. Between 2.66 and 3: identical here (leaves are all ~0.75 mm), pick 2.66 for citation alignment and
pre-register it. `origin` performed marginally best on decision agreement (27/120) but is ill-defined for a main
vessel that ends in a run-off segment and I would not adopt it on a 5-instance margin.

**Not settled by this data:** which scheme is closest to *truth* — there is no invasive FFR here; the comparison is
against the leaky model, which is itself the object under test in §2.5.

## 2. 686_right (0.864 → 0.441) — **legitimate arithmetic of the discrete structure, physiologically illegitimate; a symptom of the single-outlet degeneracy, and the single-outlet rule alone does not fix the class**

Diagnosis (`six_*.csv`, and the segment/profile dump reproduced in the probe log):
- The centreline carries **only the label RCA** — no R-PDA, no R-PLA, no acute marginal (the other nine of ten right
  trees inspected carry R-PDA and R-PLA). r_in = 1.67 mm → Murray demand 2.61 mL/s. After truncation at 0.75 mm the
  domain is one 100 mm vessel with one outlet.
- Healthy-equivalent network already loses 17 % of aortic pressure (`hout_0.75_share` = 0.830; leaky 0.944): 2.6 mL/s
  through a vessel tapering 1.67 → 0.75 mm with no shedding. C is then calibrated *small* (16.5 vs leaky 19.8) to
  make the inflow equal the demand, i.e. the microvascular bed is assigned whatever resistance is left over.
- The actual radii read 0.49–0.63 mm over arc 77–98 mm against r_ref 0.86–0.96 (four native 33–45 %DS lesions,
  K at 57.8 / 79.8 / 85.0 / 90.0 mm). Under leaky the flow there is 0.3–0.4 mL/s and the K-drop totals 1.8 mmHg;
  under discrete it is 1.29 mL/s everywhere, K-drop 19.8 mmHg and Poiseuille 30.8 mmHg, FFR 0.437. Inflow falls
  2.54 → 1.29 mL/s because the diseased network cannot pass the demand the healthy one was calibrated to.
- Solver is not at fault: converged, mass error 1e-12, five lesion terms identical under both beds.

Physiologically a single-outlet discrete RCA means "the entire right-coronary hyperaemic demand exits through the
distal 0.75 mm PDA/PL remnant", which no RCA does — the RV branches, acute marginal, PDA and PL are simply not in the
segmentation. The number is therefore set by where the label ends, not by the vessel: the same tree gives 0.85 with
the cut at 1.0 mm and 0.47 at 0.5 mm (`six_tree.csv` [minffr_1.00_share], [minffr_0.50_share]).

Cohort: **43/140 trees are single-outlet at 0.75 mm, 42 of them right** (`cohort140_tree.csv` [nout_0.75_share]).
But single-outlet is the wrong exclusion variable: those 43 have a mean offset of only −0.038, and excluding them
leaves the tail untouched (rule `nout ≥ 2`: offset −0.064, SD 0.088, min −0.433, disagreement 14/97 for share).
The offset is predicted by the **healthy-equivalent discrete network's own epicardial drop**: corr(offset,
min healthy-network FFR over resolved main nodes [hmain_0.75_share]) = **0.92**. A healthy epicardial tree that
loses > 10 % of aortic pressure at hyperaemia is not a physiological reference (normal-vessel FFR ≈ 0.94–0.97
[UNVERIFIED]), and 28/140 trees fail it. Gate `hmain ≥ 0.90` keeps 112/140 and collapses the offset to −0.029 ±
0.030 (min −0.108, disagreement 1/112, r = 0.73) for share, −0.025 ± 0.026 (disagreement 1/112, r = 0.80) for leaf3.
686_right fails this gate (0.830) and is excluded by it; so is 3_left under share (0.846).

Verdict: **exclude by the healthy-network gate, not by outlet count**; keep the outlet-count rule only where the
error type needs a branch (T1) or a non-degenerate Protocol C (one outlet + one scalar is an exact match by
construction — Protocol C collapses onto "prescribe the clean flow", cf. the T4 rows below where C ≈ 0.996 on every
single-outlet tree). Single-outlet trees remain usable for T3/T4 if they pass the gate.

## 3. Truncation radius — **0.75 mm is defensible but costs the study its side branches; recommend 0.60 mm, with 0.75 as the pre-registered fallback if Gate M1 cannot mesh 1.2 mm-diameter outlets**

`cohort140_tree.csv` / `cohort140_slot.csv`, share scheme, n = 140 trees / 121 host slots:

| cut mm | outlets median | single-outlet | zero-outlet | meas. node kept | slot keeps ≥1 downstream branch (of those that have one) | run-off kept, median | min-FFR offset vs leaky | 65 %DS offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 4 | 3 | 0 | 1.00 | 0.88 (1.00) | 116 mm | −0.046 | −0.040 |
| **0.60** | 3 | 18 | 0 | 1.00 | **0.77 (0.88)** | 107 mm | −0.048 | −0.040 |
| 0.75 | 2 | 43 | 0 | 0.99 | **0.54 (0.62)** | 87 mm | −0.056 | −0.048 |
| 0.90 | 2 | 61 | 4 | 0.83 | 0.29 (0.32) | 67 mm | −0.013 | −0.053 |
| 1.00 | 1 | 72 | 8 | 0.66 | 0.17 (0.19) | 60 mm | +0.004 | −0.056 |

Three things follow. (i) The offset is **not** a truncation artefact — the 65 %DS offset at the measurement node is
−0.040 to −0.056 at every cut, and the healthy flow at the measurement node is ×1.40–1.51 leaky at every cut
[Qh_meas/Qh_meas_leaky]; lowering the cut does not buy agreement with leaky. (The min-FFR offset shrinking to zero at
0.9–1.0 mm is the domain losing the distal nodes where the minimum sits, not agreement.) (ii) What the cut *does*
buy is T1's raison d'être: at 0.75 mm only 54 % of host slots keep a downstream branch to delete (62 % of those that
have one) and 43 trees have nothing to delete; at 0.60 mm it is 77 % (88 %) and 18 trees. (iii) Below 0.60 the gain
flattens (0.88 → 0.77 → 0.54) while the outlet radius approaches the mask's resolution: 0.60 mm radius is 1.2 mm
diameter = 3.8 in-plane voxels (0.318 mm) and 2.4 slices (0.5 mm) in `878.coronary.nii.gz`; 0.50 mm is 3.1 / 2.0.
A half-voxel error on a 0.60 mm read is ±27 % in r, ±50 % in r^2.66 — but the same error at 0.75 mm is ±21 % / ±45 %,
so the weight noise argument barely separates them.

Recommendation: **0.60 mm**, both fidelities, with the earlier review's stub rule (retained stub ≥ 3 diameters or drop
the branch in both fidelities). Pre-register 0.75 mm as the fallback conditional on Gate M1 (real-lumen pilot)
failing to mesh 1.2 mm outlets with ≥ 8 cells across. Note `R_RESOLVED` stays 0.75 mm: lesion terms and reported FFR
are still restricted to r_fit ≥ 0.75 — an outlet at 0.60 does not need to be "resolved", it needs to exist.

## 4. "Run under both structures, claim only what holds in both" — **workable for cohort-level directional claims; not workable for instance-level claims; and one pre-registered hypothesis will come out differently under the two structures, which must be reported as a result, not averaged away**

0.75 mm cut. Flip = crossing 0.80. n = 66 T1 instances (those with a downstream branch ≥ 0.75 mm), 121 T4 instances.

**T1 (missed downstream branch)**

|---|---|---|---|---|

Reading:
- **Instance level: "holds in both" is close to never satisfied.** Under B the T1 flips in the two structures are
  disjoint (0 shared of 11); under C the ΔFFR are uncorrelated or anticorrelated (r −0.28, Spearman 0.20); T4 flips
  under B are shared in 8 of 36. Any per-instance claim ("this error flips this case under both models") will fail.
- **Cohort level, directional: workable, and the direction agrees.** T1: B < A in 98–100 % of instances under
  every bed (mean |Δ| 0.047 → 0.012 leaky, 0.092 → 0.013 share); C ≈ A in both (0.082 vs 0.047 leaky; 0.091 vs 0.092
  share; flips 14 vs 21 and 28 vs 30). So H2 (re-tuning does not remove the flip hazard) survives in both, and H3
  (B ≥ A) is rejected in both. Effect sizes differ by ~2× (discrete larger), so state them per structure.
- **One qualitative disagreement, pre-registered hypothesis territory:** T4 under Protocol A moves the leaky model
  not at all (|Δ| 0.004, 2 flips) and the discrete model materially (0.022, 21 flips), because a leaky bed bypasses
  a distal calibre error through upstream leaks while a discrete bed pushes every millilitre through the undersized
  segment. "Holds in both" would delete a true finding here; it should instead be the reported BC-structure
  interaction (this is the "gift to C3" the earlier review anticipated, now with the sign the other way for T4).
- **Both structures agree that flow-matched C amplifies calibre error** (T4: |Δ| 0.146 leaky / 0.120 share; flips
  35 / 37) — the strongest cross-structure claim in the data.
- The healthy-network gate does **not** rescue instance-level agreement (T1 leaky-vs-leaf3 r on the gated subset:
  A 0.52, B 0.37, C −0.36; n = 53). The disagreement is structural, not a few pathological trees.

Verdict: keep the two-structure design, but write the claim rule as *"cohort-level direction and ordering must hold
in both; effect sizes are reported per structure; instance-level flips are never pooled across structures; a
structure-dependent result is reported as an interaction"*. Caveat: my Protocol C is a single global scalar and the
T4 error is my own definition (whole distal subtree ×0.85), so the C rows and T4 rows are indicative only.

## 5. Defects in the discrete implementation

1. **Provenance (blocking).** The decision memo's table is from the leaf-r³ variant; the committed code is share and
   gives lower numbers on every multi-outlet tree (§0). Fix: regenerate the table from the committed code, or commit
   the scheme the table came from.
2. **Silent calibration failure (real, latent).** `calibrate()` bisects log10 C in [0, 8] and walks `lo` down while
   f(lo) < 0. If the healthy-equivalent epicardial resistance alone exceeds (P_in − P_v)/Q_demand — which a
   single-outlet discrete tree approaches (686_right healthy drop is 17 % of the budget) — `lo` walks until C
   underflows to 1e-314, `_solve` returns NaN, `inflow = nan`, `min_ffr_main` reports **1.000** and `converged =
   True`. Demonstrated with `_ideal_vessel(0, r0_mm=2.5, L_mm=400, r_end_mm=0.76)` (healthy drop 111 mmHg): C =
   1e-314, Qin nan, minFFR 1.000, no exception. `severity_sweep.verify` V6 would not catch it either (`max(0.0, nan)`
   is 0.0). Not hit in the 140-tree cohort (min C over all cuts/schemes 0.47), but it will be hit by T4-type
   errors on single-outlet trees. Fix: after calibration assert the healthy solve is finite and that the pure
   epicardial inflow at C → 0 exceeds Q_demand; otherwise flag the tree ineligible.
3. **Spec/code mismatch.** CFD-ARM-SPEC §2.4 truncates where **r_fit** < 0.75; the code truncates on **r_ref** (the
   non-increasing, 1.15×-capped fit), which is more aggressive: a segment whose fit rises from 0.74 to 0.90 is
   entirely inactive. The 3D pipeline will clip on whichever the spec says; both fidelities must use one rule.
4. **Zero-outlet trees.** If no child of the root survives, `leaves = [0]`, `nr` is empty and `_solve` builds a 0×0
   system; 8/140 trees reach this at a 1.0 mm cut, 4 at 0.9 mm (`nout_1.00_share` = 0). Guard it explicitly
   (not reachable at 0.60–0.75 mm on this cohort, but it is on the sweep's refit trees, cf. V8b outlet-count change).
5. **Share scheme discontinuity at the cut (design property, must be documented if kept).** `share` splits over
   *active* children only: a sibling at 0.74 mm contributes nothing and its whole Murray share moves to the survivor.
   A T1 deletion of a branch just above the cut and the same branch just below it are therefore different
   experiments. Leaf-r³ has the same discontinuity in the weight but not in the survivor's weight, which is one more
   reason to prefer it.
6. Not a defect but worth stating in the protocol: in the discrete bed the calibrated C is smaller (median 18.7 vs
   leaky ≈ 19.8 on the six trees) because the healthy epicardial drop is larger — the microvascular resistance is a
   residual, so any comparison of "bed resistance" across structures is meaningless.
